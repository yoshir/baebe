## STRATEGY OVERVIEW

### Structural Changes: The Hobbit Framework

**Why The Hobbit Structure:**
- Episodic encounters: each chapter = distinct lesson/encounter
- Reluctant hero: Baebe doesn't want to lead, forced to
- Mentor leaves: Chapter 11 - Taivalu collapses, Baebe must lead
- Growth through experience: Baebe learns by doing
- Final confrontation: Baebe proves worth as leader

**Key Structural Additions:**
1. **Chapter 10**: First Encounter (episodic encounter #1) - **NEW**
2. **Chapter 11**: The Turning Point ("Gandalf leaves" moment) - **NEW**
3. **Chapter 12**: Parallel Journeys (forced separation for character growth) - **NEW**
4. **Chapter 13**: The Reunion (meaningful reunion, stronger bonds) - **NEW**
5. **Chapter 14**: Temple Trials (moved from Chapter 10, now after reunion)

**Character Introduction Changes:**
- **Chapter 4**: Baebe fully broken (no rescue yet) - **NEW EMPHASIS**

### Character Voice Changes

**Taivalu's Voice:**
- **OLD**: Verbose, gentle teacher, talks a lot
- **NEW**: Brief, poetic, Roy Batty style (few words, high impact)
- **Style**: "I've felt the frequencies fade. Like a song ending before the final note."
- **No terms of endearment**: No "darling," "love," etc.
- **Philosophical but brief**: "Time is just another frequency. We have what we need."

**Baebe's Voice:**
- **OLD**: Tactical, serious, no humor
- **NEW**: Deadpan military humor (literal responses to poetry)
- **Style**: "Song is not a movement order. Move."
- **Contrast humor**: Poetic prophecy → Tactical response
- **Example**: Taivalu: "Like stardust returning to the void..." Baebe: "Stardust is non-tactical. Focus on survival protocols."

**Natural Banter:**
- **NOT forced comedy**: Character-driven interactions
- **Taivalu/Baebe**: Poetic vs. tactical contrast
- **Kess**: Child perspective (lighter moments, not comedy)
- **Shadow**: Physical communication (no dialogue)
- **Angel**: Guilty, complex (no comedy)
