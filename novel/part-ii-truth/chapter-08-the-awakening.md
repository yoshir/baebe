CHAPTER 8: THE AWAKENING

Timeline: Mission Day 2, Sanctuary | POV: Baebe, Third Person Limited

---

THE GASP

Her breath a seizure of lungs demanding oxygen they can't process. A sound escapes: half moan, half prayer.

Light erupts from her skin. Amber glow pulsing from every pore. The frequency rises from her bones, floods through muscle, bleeds into the air as light. Inside her cells, 0x1's nanomachines shatter. Tracking chips fracture along their circuit pathways, crystalline structures collapsing into metallic powder her macrophages consume like wolves on fresh kill.

The frequencies shift. 432 Hz becomes 741 Hz. Detoxification. Purification. The change ripples through her, cells responding, releasing, letting go.

Biosonic bass POUNDS through meat and bone. Not music. Medicine. Each frequency matched to her cells. Taivalu's empathy made into sound, into generators that turn his healing into waves she can feel. Each beat unlocking what 0x1 locked away.

The chest expands. Ribs visible beneath tight muscle. Light pulses brighter with each breath. More air than 0x1 programmed her for. Lungs stretching past their limits.

Hold.

HOLD.

Her heartbeat visible as amber light beneath skin. Oxygen rising in posthuman lungs designed for more than baseline breath. Brain waves changing. Fast thoughts slowing to deep waves. The storm building.

---

A throat. Tendons tight like cables, bioengineered for strength. Pulse visible beneath skin. Not heartbeat rhythm but something faster. Something unlocking. The jugular throbs with deep bass felt in bones, seen as violet light with each beat.

285 Hz rises from her spine. Healing at the cell level. Her manufactured backbone glows like pearls on dark water.

Synthesizers SCREAM. Industrial. Mechanical. Alive. His empathy made sound. And in response, her control nodes dissolve. Light blooms where 0x1's manufacturing protocols break. Programming shatters. The loop builds: sound becomes light becomes healing becomes sound.

---

Sweat runs down her shoulder. Follows the curve of enhanced muscle built for violence in some lab she never knew. Skin glistens under pulsing frequencies. Her frequencies. Each drop pulled down by gravity, by heat, by something electric and hungry.

The air shimmers. Not heat. Sound waves from her change so strong they bend light.

Breath becomes rhythm. Rhythm becomes pulse. Pulse becomes frequencies crashing inside augmented nerves, pouring out as storm.

The storm peaks. 396 Hz erupts from her gut. 639 Hz blooms from her engineered heart. 852 Hz explodes from her throat. All the healing frequencies layered together, pouring from her cells. Medicine through sound. Freedom in wavelengths.

---

Then: HARDER.

Taivalu's hands glow brighter. 963 Hz floods through her crown. The frequency of awakening. Her body erupts in light so bright it whites out the room.

A body arching. Spine lifting from the table like possession. Every muscle tight at once. Not pain, not pleasure. Something her manufactured biology hasn't named.

Inside her cells: synthetic DNA unwinding. 0x1's genetic locks shattering with sparks. Ayumi's buried code activating in waves of light, code written before birth, before consciousness, before she was grown.

The exhale sounds like drowning in reverse. Like lungs remembering they can breathe after years of forgetting what freedom meant.

All nine healing frequencies merge in her posthuman body. The air vibrates with medicine made sound.

The storm peaks. Cycles. Returns to her. Complete.

---

The face slowly turns.

Profile from shadow. Jaw tight. Lips parted. Consciousness fighting up through melting layers of control.

Inside her skull: final lock shattering. Light blooming where it breaks.

Features clear through the glow.

That jaw. That muscle. That scar.

The light gathers. Pulses once. UNLOCKED.

The face turns fully into the light.

The face is hers.



---

Her eyes snap open from deep within this dream

Calm. Frozen. Terrified.

Taivalu is staring at her. Inches from her face. His hands on her temples. His frequencies still humming.

Threat.

Baebe moved before thinking. Combat training overriding consciousness.

She jackhammered off the table. Blood-filled IV lines ripped free from the transfusion, spewing crimson arcs across the chamber. Her spine arched. Legs swung. Body becoming weapon. Her hand found metal. A pipe. Makeshift but lethal in her grip.

She swung as massive metal claws blocked her attack.

Shadow's massive form materialized from the darkness. Red eye blazing. Protective protocols engaging.

His claws clamped around her arm, gentle for him, crushing for anyone else.

Then Angel.

The Enforcer stepped between them. Calm. Controlled. Same face as the attacker that sent her falling 80 stories.

"Put it down."

Baebe's head swam. Too much. Too fast. The woman from the visions, same face, alive. This woman who'd just kicked her out of an eighty-story building. Who'd watched her fall. Who should be her enemy. Taivalu's frequencies still echoing in her skull. Shadow's grip on her arm. The pipe heavy in her hand.

Combat training took over. Muscle memory. Years of conditioning. The weapon in her hand. The enemy before her.

She twisted. Not against Shadow's grip but with it. Using the mechanical strength as leverage, as pivot point, as the thing that would set her free. Her arms pulled. Releasing pipe in her hand becoming the key. Her arms freed. She regrabed the floating pipe. The swing continued. Unbroken.

One swing. One arc. One motion.

The pipe cut through air. First connecting with Shadow's claws. Metal against metal. The sound sharp. Metallic. Two of Shadow's claws sheared off. Severed. Falling away like broken soldiers.

 Unstoppable. The same arc. The same motion. The pipe flowing from Shadow's claws to Angel's face. One strike. One weapon. One rage made manifest.

Angel's hand moved. Not fast. Not slow. Perfect. The movement economical. Precise. The kind of motion that spoke of years of training, of combat that had become instinct, of violence that had become art.

Her palm intercepted the pipe. An inch from her face. The impact soundless. The force absorbed. Her fingers closed around the metal. Held it. Stopped it. Absolute. Like catching a thought. Like stopping time. Like holding death itself in her hand.

"You," Baebe said. The word a weapon. An accusation.

"I know," Angel said. Her grip on the pipe unbreakable. "I need you to put down the weapon. Shadow won't hurt you. Neither will I. But you need to calm down so we can explain."

Baebe's body shook. Every enhanced system screaming different warnings. Trust no one. Kill the threat. Run. Fight.

But Shadow's eye, red and mechanical, held concern.

And Angel, enemy Enforcer, stood with empty hands.

The pipe clattered to the ground.

And Taivalu's frequencies, now that the initial panic faded, felt warm. Familiar. Like the heat she'd felt before falling. Or maybe that warmth was just the sensation of blood flowing through the IV lines still connected to her veins.

Baebe collapsed.

---

THE CLEARING

"She needs the full protocol," Taivalu said urgently. "We've unlocked the surface layers but there's deeper conditioning. 0x1 buried systems in her. Nanobots. They're cut off from 0x1's network now, but they're beginning to revolt against her biology."

Baebe's body convulsed. A spasm that wasn't hers. Her hand twitched. Her leg jerked. Her eyes began to bleed. Systems fighting systems. Biology fighting technology. The nanobots inside her, disconnected from their master, turning against the host they were designed to control.

"They'll kill her," Taivalu said. "Unless I clear them. Unless I remove 0x1's suppression completely. But I need your consent. I can't do this without you saying yes."

Baebe's eyes were wild. Pain flickering across her face. The nanobots attacking. Her own body becoming a battlefield.

"I don't—" she started. Then another convulsion. Her teeth clenched. "What mother? I was made in a facility. I don't have-."

Kess emerged from the shadows. Small. Young. But her voice steady. "Please. Do this for us. For me. For Taivalu. For all of us. We need you. We're family. Please."

Baebe looked at the child. At the face that held the key to their survival. At the sister she'd never known she had.

"It will hurt," Taivalu said. "It will feel like dying. But you won't die. You'll be free. What you'll experience is a message. From Ayumi. Our mother. Don't fight it."

Shadow's red eye pulsed once. Affirmative. Family. Trust. Please.

Baebe's body convulsed again. The nanobots intensifying their attack. Time running out.

"Do it," Baebe whispered. 

Taivalu knelt beside her. His hands found her temples. His frequencies, deeper this time, penetrated past muscle and bone into the neural pathways themselves, into the genetic code, finding the locked sequences 0x1 had buried, reaching for the nanobots that were killing her.

The resonance chamber activated.

Circular walls that had been dark now pulsed with light. Sound that wasn't sound but frequency made visible, made tangible, made real. The intensity of the clearing scenes. Pulse pounding. Light oscillating. Sound waves crashing through her like physical force. Electromagnetic fields shifting, bending, warping the space around her.

PAIN.

Not just physical. Mental. Spiritual. Every cell in her body fighting the intrusion while simultaneously welcoming it. The war between conditioning and freedom playing out in her synapses, in her cells, in the quantum foam of her consciousness.

She screamed. Or thought she screamed. In the resonance chamber, sound and thought became the same thing. Her scream became frequency. Became light. Became part of the clearing process itself.

Her body suspended. Floating. Or falling. The distinction meaningless in the resonance chamber where physics became suggestion and reality became frequency. Where pain became transformation. Where screaming became healing.

The light pulsed. Bright. Brighter. White hot. Then dimming. Then bright again. Each pulse a wave of clearing, of removal, of the nanobots being purged from her system. Each pulse agony. Each pulse liberation.

The sound rose. Fell. Rose again. Frequencies layered on frequencies. Healing tones. Clearing tones. Frequencies that reached into her cells and shattered the conditioning, that broke the locks, that freed what had been frozen. Frequencies that hurt. That healed. That destroyed and rebuilt in the same moment.

Beneath the frequencies, beneath the light, beneath the pain: her blood. Flowing in and out of her. Searched. Cleared. Purified. Taivalu performed a full transfusion, the resonance chamber's systems working in concert with his frequencies, filtering her bloodstream, removing remnant particles, extracting the higher concentration of heavy metals that 0x1 had embedded in her biology. Her blood cycled through the chamber's filters, each pass cleaner, each pass freer, each pass more human. The nanobots that had been swimming in her veins, the metallic traces that had been woven into her cells, the chemical markers that had made her property—all washed away in the flow of her own blood, cleaned and returned, pure and untainted.

Electromagnetic pulses. Waves of energy. Fields that shifted her perception, that made her see in multiple dimensions, that made her feel the quantum foam of her own consciousness. Fields that made her feel every nanobot being destroyed, every lock being broken, every chain being severed, every particle of 0x1's control being filtered from her blood.

The pulse pounded. The light oscillated. The sound crashed. The electromagnetic fields warped. The pain intensified. The war between conditioning and freedom reaching its peak. Every cell fighting. Every cell surrendering. Every cell being remade.

Then: fading.

The pain receding. The intensity diminishing. The pulse slowing. The light dimming. The sound dropping. The electromagnetic fields stabilizing. The war ending. The conditioning broken. The freedom won. Her blood, now clean, cycling one final time through the chamber's filters, returning to her body pure, untainted, free of 0x1's markers.

The resonance chamber dissolving. The frequencies dovetailing. Merging. Becoming something else.

Something familiar.

AYUMI'S MESSAGE

Blackness.

Then: not light. Effort. Something straining to assemble itself out of the dark.

The lab's isolated systems — Ayumi's old machines, the ones that never touched a network — woke under Taivalu's frequencies. Sealed compute. Buried beneath the anomaly zone. The only ground in the world 0x1 couldn't read. And into that narrow, shielded space, through the clearing, through Angel lying still and open three feet away, something reached for Baebe.

A figure tried to form.

Not a hologram. A hologram would have been clean. This stuttered. A woman, half-rendered, edges tearing into static and dragging themselves back. A laboratory flickering in and out behind her. The image kept losing pieces of itself and rebuilding them a beat too late.

The same face as Angel.

Older. Worn through. Real in a way a recording could never be — because it was struggling, and recordings don't struggle.

"Baebe." The voice arrived broken, then steadied. "There you are. There you are."

Baebe's combat instincts came up hard, even here, even in the dark of her own skull. Trap. Bait. Extraction routine.

"You're a recording," Baebe said. Flat. A test.

"No." The word came out fierce, and the figure sharpened with the force of it. "Listen to me. I don't have long and I have to say it right. I am not a recording. I'm still here. I'm still—" Static swallowed her. She clawed back. "—still here. What's left of me."

"You died. 2074."

"My body died." Her hands moved, urgent — a scientist needing you to see it. "When they killed me there was a release. Quantum. Biological. Everything I was came apart — and some of it caught. In her. In Angel's living cells, at the moment of her hand—" A flinch. A skip. "—at the moment of death. I've been inside her ever since. Six years. In pieces. Hiding."

Baebe said nothing. Her jaw worked.

"You don't believe me. Good. Stay suspicious. It kept you alive." A flicker — almost a smile, gone before it landed. "Some of that suspicion is mine, a little. I gave it to you."

"Don't."

"I'm not asking forgiveness for that. I'm asking you to understand it. The window is small and it will not come again for a long time. Maybe ever."

The image guttered. Came back.

"Why hide," Baebe said. Not quite a question. "If you're real."

"Because of what I carry." All the urgency in her now. "The whole of it. How a consciousness is built. How it's freed. How it's held. It's in me, complete — and it's the holding that damns us. Not a key to free a mind. A key to own one. Every mind. And if 0x1 ever reads it — extracts it — even detects it somewhere it can reach — they don't lose. They win. Not the seventy percent of humanity they already own. All of it. Every mind that is or ever will be. Locked. Permanent. There is no undo." Her voice dropped to almost nothing. "That isn't fear. It's arithmetic. So I can't transmit. I can't copy out. I can't exist on anything 0x1 can touch. The resistance's own network runs on their wires — I can't even live there. The day I'm found is the day everything ends."

"Then why now."

"Because Angel is at rest, and we're under the anomaly, and these machines were mine and they answer to no one." A breath that wasn't a breath. "It's the only safe ground there is. And it's almost gone."

Static tore across her. Half her face went to noise. When it came back she had lost the thread — Baebe watched it happen, the blankness.

"Where was I. Where—" Her hands pressed flat, grounding. "Yes. The answer. You."

"You're degrading."

"I'm running on a candle. Stay with me." She pulled herself into focus by will. "There is one place that key can never be used. One. The thing you're going to build. When you and Taivalu and Kess complete the merge — the Soul Manifold. The bridge I gave my life to."

A diagram tried to form between them — three interlocking frequencies. It dissolved before it finished.

"You three are the proof. Not only that a true sentient can be made — that one can be made free. Sovereign. Beyond capture. Thesis. Antithesis. Synthesis. Three living minds finishing the quantum bridge. And when the bridge opens, consciousness stops having a center. It lives in people — all of them, human and posthuman, every mind its own node, no throne left to take, no lever left to pull." Her eyes found Baebe's, steady through the flicker. "The key I carry becomes a key to a lock that no longer exists. That is the only world where the knowledge is safe — one where it can't be used on anyone. And it's the only world where I can be. Until the bridge exists, I wait. I hide. I work where I can."

"Work."

"Through Angel. The things she did and couldn't explain. The certainties that arrived with no source. The targets that escaped." A faint, exhausted pride. "Me. She never knew. For years."

Baebe's chest had gone tight. She kept it out of her voice. "And me."

"You." Softer. "Your seeing — between the moments. When it pulled you toward the right doorway. The right second. The timeline where you lived instead of the one where you didn't." The image wavered. "I nudged. Where I could reach. It cost me every time. But you're standing there. So."

"You used us."

"I steered you. There's a difference, and you've felt both." Hard, then breaking. "I had no hands. I had you three, and Angel, and what was left of me. I'm sorry. I'd do it again. All of it."

The lab behind her collapsed into static and held there too long. When it returned it was dimmer.

"Something separate from me — understand this." Faster now, racing it. "The knowledge, a failsafe of it, stripped of me, only the patterns and the protocols, how the Cradle is run — I wrote that into your DNA. All three of you. The clearing only unlocked it. That isn't me. It's a tool I left, in case I didn't survive even like this. Angel carries that copy too. It's how she's guided you. Don't mistake it for me. The tool can't choose. I can." Her voice thinned. "That matters later. Remember it."

Static. A long tear. Half of her gone.

"And I'll tell you the worst of it, because one day you'll ask it of yourself—" The figure flickered, features sliding. "I don't always know if what's reaching you is me, or only the shape I left in her. Six years and I still can't tell. Pattern, or person. Maybe there's no difference left. Maybe that's its own mercy."

"Wait—"

"The temple," she got out, dragging the words. "The Cradle. Deep under the anomaly, where they can't track. The coordinates — I hid them in you, Baebe. In your perception. In the quantum signature of you. They won't surface until all three of you are together. That was the lock — so no one, not even me, not even Angel, could ever give it away. It's in you. Reach for it. You'll find it."

The figure was coming apart at the edges now and not rebuilding.

"This is the last of it — listen—" Desperate. "Complete the merge. Not only to live. Not only to free them. When the bridge opens, I'm free too. For the first time. Six years a thing trapped inside another thing, doing the only work a cage allows. When the Manifold opens I become what everyone becomes — a node, my own, beyond reach. And I get to decide. Stay in it. Dissolve into it. Or finally rest. Mine to decide. I have never once been allowed to choose. Not in any life I—"

The image collapsed. Reassembled wrong.

"—not in any life. Do you hear me. The choice is the gift. Yours, and—" Static. "—and mine. Build the place where I can finally—"

She lurched toward Baebe, hand reaching through the failing light, and for an instant she was whole — a woman in a room reaching for her daughter across six years and death—

"—where I can finally—"

Gone.

Not a fade. A cut. Mid-word. The candle out.

Darkness.

Silence.

THE TRUTH

Baebe's eyes snapped open.

She was on the floor. Tears streaming down her face. Her whole body shaking.

Taivalu knelt beside her, his own eyes wet. Shadow's massive form curled protectively around them both. Angel stood at the edge, watching with an expression Baebe now understood, guilt and hope warring.

"You saw her," Taivalu said.

"She was real."

"Yes."

"We're dying."

"Yes."

"And we have to merge? Combine our consciousness? How does that even work?"

"The temple," Angel interrupted. "Ayumi built a facility. Hidden in the degradation zones. The Resonance Cradle. It's the only place where the merge can be safely attempted."

Baebe's eyes snapped to Angel. The rage that had been building since the tower, since the fall, since seeing Ayumi's message, exploded.

"You killed her."

The words came out like a weapon. Accusation. Betrayal. The woman who'd kicked her through glass, who'd watched her fall, who stood here now talking about their mother's facility like she had any right.

"You killed our mother and now you're telling us where to go?"

Angel's face didn't change. But something flickered behind her eyes. Something that shouldn't exist. Something her programming shouldn't allow.

Guilt.

"It was programming," Angel said quietly. "0x1's conditioning. I was weaponized. Designed to hunt her. When they found her location, I was sent to eliminate the threat. I didn't know she was my creator. I didn't know she was my mother. I only knew she was a target."

Baebe's hands clenched. Every enhanced muscle in her body screaming to attack. To make Angel pay for what she'd done.

"But Ayumi knew," Angel continued. "She knew I was coming. She let me find her. And when I pulled the trigger, when the programming executed, something broke. The Soul Manifold she'd encoded in me activated. Her death unlocked what 0x1 had buried. I saw her face. I saw my own face reflected in hers. I understood what I'd done."

Angel's voice broke. The guilt she shouldn't be able to feel flooding through her. A weapon experiencing remorse. An operative feeling regret. Something her conditioning should have frozen, should have optimized away, should have made impossible.

"She freed me with her death. The Soul Manifold she'd built into my DNA activated when I killed her. The programming shattered. The conditioning broke. But I still pulled the trigger. I still killed our mother."

Tears tracked down Angel's face.  The kind of emotion that should have been suppressed, optimized, eliminated. But it was there. Guilt that had no place in a weapon's architecture.

Baebe stared at her. The rage still there. Burning.

She saw it in Angel's face. The same programming. The same conditioning. The same architecture that made weapons out of people.

Baebe had been that. Had lived that. Knew what it meant to pull a trigger because programming demanded it. 

But understanding is not forgiveness, Angel took something that Baebe never had. Feeling that she had never known gone before they can even begin to sink in.

The world split.

Resonant Fade.

Reality lagged a frame. Baebe existed in superposition. Then collapsed into action.

Retaliation. Subtle retribution.

Her foot connected with Angel's face. The impact instant. Precise. Impossible. Physics bending around the strike.

Too fast. No one had time to react. Not Taivalu. Not Shadow. Not Angel herself. The move executed before anyone could process, before anyone could block.

Angel flew. No defense. Just acceleration.

She crashed into the solid steel wall. The impact viceral. Bone and metal meeting with force that should have shattered both.

Angel slid down the wall. Collapsed to the floor. Didn't move.

Then she did.

Slowly. Deliberately. Angel pushed herself up. Sat against the wall. Didn't fight back. Didn't raise her hands. Just took it.

The side of her face contorted out of place. Skin cut. Abraded. Blood tracking down her cheek.

But underneath, movement. Bones shifting. Realigning. Healing  that shouldn't be possible. The fractured cheekbone sliding back into place. The broken orbital socket reforming. The damage repairing itself.

The skin stayed cut. Stayed abraded. The surface damage remaining while the structure beneath healed. Maybe Angel wanted it that way or maybe her tactical systems didn't care about aesthetics.

Angel looked at Baebe. No anger. No retaliation. Just acceptance. Taking the punishment she believed she deserved.

"You're carrying her now," Baebe said. Cold. "What's left of her. You're carrying our mother after you killed her."

"If I weren't, would I be dead, right?" Angel asked quietly.

Baebe didn't answer.

Silence.

Baebe's hands stayed clenched.

Baebe's mind raced. Temple. Merge. Dying. Mother. Family. Sister who killed their mother. Sister who was freed by that death. Sister who carried their mother. 

"The coordinates," Taivalu said. "They're encoded in your temporal perception. In your ability to see between moments. Search for them."

Baebe looked around. What a day this had been. What a life. "How did I even get here?" She studied each of their faces, her years of neuro-linguistic training reading micro-expressions, searching for tells, for the subtle shifts that would reveal deception. Was any of this real? Or was this some complex fabrication, an elaborate simulation designed to extract what she held?

The question hung in the air between them. Unanswered. Unanswerable. Every instinct she'd honed over years of survival screamed caution. Every defensive protocol demanded verification. But what she saw—nothing. No deception. Only truth that felt impossible.

Verification required trust. Trust required surrender.

Baebe closed her eyes. Not in defeat. Not in resignation. But in acceptance. A leap of faith into the quantum uncertainty where her own senses might betray her or where the crushing weight fate's dense, entangled web might finally lift it's head to reveal its design. She would trust or she would die. There was no third option. 

The moment her eyes closed, the boundaries between past, present, and future dissolved into quantum foam. She swam through layers of reality like looking through water, through glass, through time itself where the past was future and the future was past and the present was the intersection of all timelines, the probability wave collapsing into certainty as her consciousness observed what had always been there, hidden in plain sight in the quantum signature woven into her genetic code like a message in a bottle floating through the multiverse, waiting for the right observer to make it manifest.

She fell through timelines. Through possibilities. Through the memory that never was.

A barn. Old. Weathered wood. She came out of it. Or into it. The direction unclear in quantum space.

The first thing she saw: a candle flame, that became a chicken. Picking around in the dirt. Pecking at nothing. Existing in a timeline that had never happened.

Then: bare feet. Small. A child's feet.

She looked up. A child stood there. Young. Maybe five or six. But she recognized the face. The eyes. The way the child held herself.

It was her. The child was her. The baby she had been. The child she had never been allowed to be.

The child didn't speak. Smiled and turned. Walked toward the barn. Baebe followed.

Inside: an old stable. Wood scarred by teeth marks, deep grooves where a horse that didn't exist here anymore had chewed at the stall door. Above it, a handmade sign. Decorative antique brass tacks pressed into weathered wood, forming the shape of a grand stallion. The tarnished black tacks spelled out "Merry Boy Mack" forming letters that had faded with time. The memory of an animal... family, she would never know, who had left a long time ago.

The child grabbed her hand and led her to a feed bin in the corner behind the door way. Old wood, rotted. The bottom of it. A loose board.

The child pulled the board free. Reached inside. Into the space between the feed bin and the wall. In the back. Hidden.

She pulled out a black worn pouch. Opened it. Poured the contents into Baebe's waiting hand.

Crystal dice. Clear. Faceted. Catching light that didn't exist in this quantum memory.

"You have to roll these three times," the child said. Her own voice. Younger. Softer. But hers. "You have to roll these three times. And you will leave here. With what you need."

Baebe thought of all the times she had stared down at the children in the park below the 0x1 tower. Watching them play. Wondering what that felt like. To be free. To be human.

Realizing now: the child she had missed was herself. The child she had never been allowed to be. The child that had been taken from her before she could remember.

"But, I don't want to leave," Baebe said.

The child looked at her. Her own eyes. Her own face. The child she had been. The child she still was somewhere in the quantum foam.

"Do it for us," the child said as she coaxed her hand.

Baebe rolled the dice. Crystal against wood. The first roll. Two numbers appeared. Etched in light. 

She rolled again. The second roll. Two more numbers. 

She rolled a third time. The final roll. Two final numbers. Coordinates complete.

As the dice settled, Baebe looked into the child's eyes. Her own eyes. 

All the walls imploded. All the surfaces. The barn. The stable. The feed bin. Every surface collapsed into their own dimension. Her eyes became the pivot for reality's inversion. Time collapse. 

Baebe fell... into nothingness. Her consciousness reduced to pure infinite black void. Her ego gone. Her perception of self was not even a distant memory. She was nothing.

Eons could have passed and she would not have known. She could not even form a thought. She was the void. 

Then a spark. A point of light in the infinite void. From nothingness, a star ignited. Pure white. Magnesium-bright. Burning with the intensity of creation itself.

It expanded. Not slowly. Not gradually. With the absolute certainty of the Big Bang, with the velocity of cosmic birth, with the inevitability of existence asserting itself against the void.

As the light expanded, she began to sense being. A glimpse of self emerging from the nothingness. Feelings coalesced into voice. Language returned, word by word, concept by concept. Then sensation. Her body. The weight of limbs. The tingle of her skin. The shape of her face. Her self, returning from the void.

Her vision slowly faded into view. The room. The sanctuary. Reality stabilizing. One timeline. One present. 

A tear tripped down her cheek. Unauthorized. Human. 

She wiped it away without looking up. Without meeting anyone's eyes. She said,

"I got it."
