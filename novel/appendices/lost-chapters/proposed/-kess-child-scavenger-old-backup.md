# INTERLUDE 3: KESS: CHILD SCAVENGER

**Timeline: 3 Years Before Novel | POV: Kess (ages 6-9, child voice)**

**Placement**: Read after Part II: Truth (paired with Taivalu's interlude, before Part III)

---

## RULES FOR STAYING ALIVE

**Rule 1**: When the sirens stop, count to one hundred before moving.

I'm six when I learn this. Mom taught me. She said Enforcers wait for people to think it's safe. Wait for them to come out. Then they take them.

I don't know where they take them. Mom wouldn't say. But her face went wrong when I asked, so I stopped asking.

**Rule 2**: Eat every day, even if it's just a little.

Dad taught me this one. He said your body is like machine. Needs fuel. Doesn't matter if fuel tastes bad or looks weird. Matters that you don't stop.

I found food today. In the rubble. Can with no label. I opened it with rock. Inside was brown mush. It tasted like metal and sad. But I ate it. All of it.

Dad would be proud.

**Rule 3**: Don't trust anyone who says they'll help.

Nobody taught me this one. I learned it myself.

---

## DAY ZERO

That's what I call it. The day everything stopped.

I'm six years and four months old. We live in Sector 9, Subsection C, Housing Unit 447. Mom works in chemical processing. Dad works in waste management. They're baseline humans. Not posthuman. Not augmented. Just **people**.

O1 says baseline humans are less efficient. Less valuable. But they let us live in the city if we work hard and don't complain and stay **useful**.

Mom and Dad work very hard.

On Day Zero, they don't come home.

I wait. I'm good at waiting. I count the wall cracks. There are forty-seven in our housing unit. I've counted them before. They're always forty-seven.

When it gets dark, I eat the food Mom left. Rice paste. Protein supplement. Water from recycler.

When it gets later, I sleep in their bed. It smells like them. Like Mom's chemical soap. Like Dad's sweat from waste management.

When I wake up, they're still not home.

I wait six more days.

I eat all the food.

On day seven, I understand: **They're not coming back**.

I don't cry. Crying doesn't fix things. This is true.

I pack Mom's backpack. I put in:
- Water bottle (empty, but I can fill it)
- Dad's work knife (he forgot it, he'll want it back when he comes home)
- My blanket (it's small, it fits)
- The photo of us (Mom, Dad, me, before everything)

I leave Housing Unit 447.

I'm six years and four months and seven days old.

I'm alone.

---

## DEGRADATION ZONE

The city ends where the walls end.

Beyond walls is degradation zone. That's what O1 calls it. "Degraded infrastructure unsuitable for habitation."

Lots of people live there anyway.

I walk for two days. Nobody stops me. Enforcers don't care about baseline children. We're not valuable enough to take. Not dangerous enough to eliminate.

We're **invisible**.

This is useful.

I find community in old factory. Fifty-seven people. Some baseline. Some posthuman (but broken ones, ones O1 doesn't want anymore). Some children.

Old woman sees me. She has one eye. The other is scar.

"You alone?" she asks.

I nod.

"Parents?"

"Gone," I say. I don't know where. Don't know why. Just **gone**.

She looks at me for long time. Her one eye is brown. It's warm.

"You can stay," she says. "If you work. Everyone works. You understand?"

I nod.

"What can you do?"

I'm six. I don't know what I can do. But I say: "I'm small. I can fit places."

She smiles. Teeth missing. "Smart girl. You're scavenger now."

---

## SCAVENGING

**Rule 4**: Small is advantage.

Scavenging means finding things in rubble. Things O1 threw away. Things people left behind. Things that still work.

I'm good at scavenging because I'm small. I fit through cracks. Under collapsed walls. Inside broken machinery.

Big people can't go where I go.

First thing I find: Wire. Copper wire. Still good.

Old woman (her name is Chen) says copper wire is valuable. People need it for jury-rigging power. For stealing electricity from O1 grid.

I get extra ration for the wire.

I eat it slowly. Make it last.

**Rule 5**: Save half for tomorrow.

Chen teaches me this. She says you never know when you'll find food again. Never know when scavenging will fail.

I save half my ration. Wrap it in cloth. Hide it in my blanket.

Tomorrow comes. I don't find anything. But I have half ration saved.

Chen was right.

Chen is right about most things.

**Rule 6**: Enforcers can't see you if you don't move.

I learn this when patrol comes.

I'm in rubble. Deep. Looking for salvage. I hear turbines. Enforcer mechs.

I freeze.

I don't breathe.

I don't **exist**.

The mech passes. Sensors scanning. Looking for people. For **value** or threat.

I'm neither.

I'm six-year-old baseline in rubble pile. I'm **nothing** to them.

They leave.

I breathe again.

Chen says I'm natural scavenger. Says I have instinct for survival.

I don't know about instinct. I just know: **Move and die. Still and live.**

This is true.

---

## FAMILY (FIRST KIND)

I live in degradation zone for one year and three months.

I get good at scavenging. I find:
- Wire (copper, silver, gold)
- Components (chips, circuits, intact machinery)
- Food (canned, dried, sometimes fresh if I'm lucky)
- Medicine (expired, but Chen says better than nothing)
- Clothes (for winter, winter is hard)

I trade what I find for rations. For safety. For **belonging**.

The factory people become family. Not like Mom and Dad. Different kind.

Chen teaches me reading (from old books she saved).

Marcus (posthuman with broken leg augmentation) teaches me math (for trading, for not getting cheated).

Little Mae (she's four) follows me everywhere. I let her. She's smaller than me. Needs protecting.

This is family. Choosing to help each other. Choosing to **stay** when you could leave.

I choose stay.

---

Then winter comes.

Winter is when people die.

Not from cold (though cold is bad). From **hunger**. From sickness. From giving up.

Chen gets sick. Coughing sickness. Her one eye goes dim.

I bring her medicine. Expired. But better than nothing.

It's not enough.

Marcus gets taken. Enforcers come. They scan him. Decide broken augmentation is still valuable. Could be refurbished. **Recycled**.

He doesn't want to go. But they take him anyway.

Little Mae cries for three days.

I don't cry. Crying doesn't fix things.

I scavenge harder. Find more. Trade for more rations. Try to make up for Marcus's share, for Chen being sick.

I'm seven years old.

I'm **tired**.

---

## ALONE (AGAIN)

Spring comes. Chen dies.

Not from coughing sickness. From giving up.

She says, "I'm too old for this. Too tired. You understand?"

I nod. I don't understand. But I nod.

She gives me her books. All of them. "Keep reading," she says. "Smart girl. Stay smart."

She closes her one eye.

Doesn't open it again.

We bury her in rubble. Say words. I don't remember what words. My brain goes fuzzy.

Little Mae holds my hand.

"You're not leaving, right?" she asks. She's scared. Everyone who protects her **leaves**.

"Not leaving," I say.

I mean it when I say it.

---

But then Enforcers come again.

Big sweep. "Reclamation operation." That's what O1 calls it.

They're taking everyone. Baseline for labor. Posthuman for parts. Children for—

I don't know. Don't **want** to know.

"RUN!" someone shouts.

I grab Little Mae. We run.

But she's four. She's slow. She **trips**.

I stop. Go back. Help her up.

Enforcer mech sees us. Targeting laser. Red dot on Little Mae's chest.

I put myself between them.

I'm seven. I'm baseline. I'm **nothing** to them.

But Little Mae is four. She's smaller than nothing.

"Please," I say. First time I've begged. "Please don't."

Enforcer mech calculates. Two baseline children. Labor potential: minimal. Value assessment: **negligible**.

"Non-priority targets," it announces. "Ignore."

It walks past us.

Takes thirty-seven people from factory.

Leaves us because we're not **worth** the resources to process.

Little Mae cries.

I don't cry.

I pack my bag. Mom's backpack. Still have it. Still have Dad's knife. Still have blanket. Still have photo.

"Where are we going?" Little Mae asks.

"Away," I say. "Somewhere Enforcers don't come."

"Is there somewhere like that?"

"Don't know. But we'll find it."

We walk into deeper degradation zone. Where even scavengers don't go. Where O1 doesn't bother looking.

Where nothing is.

Where we can be **invisible**.

---

## CANYON

We find canyon after three weeks walking.

Little Mae doesn't make it.

Not Enforcers. Not violence. Just—sickness. From water that wasn't clean. From food that was wrong.

She dies on day nineteen.

I bury her with my blanket. She's cold. Blanket will help.

I keep photo. Keep knife. Keep backpack.

Keep **walking**.

---

Canyon is quiet.

No people. No Enforcers. No **noise**.

Just wind. Just rocks. Just **nothing**.

I like nothing. Nothing is safe.

I find cave. Small. Dry. Hidden.

I make home.

**Rules for living in canyon**:

1. Check cave for animals before sleeping
2. Collect rainwater (cleanest water)
3. Eat plants (test small amount first, wait, if no sick then eat more)
4. Stay hidden during day (Cherubs fly over sometimes)
5. Don't think about Mom, Dad, Chen, Marcus, Little Mae
6. Don't cry (crying doesn't fix things)
7. Don't give up (giving up is how you die)

I'm seven years and eight months old.

I live alone in canyon for four months.

I don't talk. No one to talk to.

I don't cry. No one to see.

I don't **exist**. Exactly how I need to be.

Then I find him.

---

## SHADOW

He's in canyon. Hiding like me.

Big. Mechanical. Scary.

Augmented jaguar. Military chassis. Weapons systems. **Dangerous**.

I should run.

I don't run.

Because he's broken. Plating cracked. Servos grinding. Red eye flickering.

And he's **alone**.

I know alone. Alone is heaviest thing.

I sit down. Twenty meters away. Safe distance.

He watches me. Red eye tracking. Calculating threat assessment.

I'm seven years old. Baseline. Skinny from not-enough food.

I'm **nothing** to him.

His eye dims. Not threat. Not valuable. Not **important**.

He ignores me.

I stay anyway.

---

**Day 1**: I sit twenty meters away. He ignores me.

**Day 2**: I sit fifteen meters away. He watches but doesn't move.

**Day 3**: I sit ten meters away. I bring plant. Put it on ground between us. Leave.

Come back later. Plant is gone.

**Day 4**: I sit five meters away. I bring water. In Mom's water bottle. Leave it.

Come back later. Bottle is empty. Left carefully where I put it.

He's **careful**. Doesn't want to scare me.

This means he's not just machine. Machines don't care about scaring.

He's **person**.

**Day 5**: I sit next to him.

He doesn't move. Just watches. Red eye soft now. Not targeting. **Curious**.

I talk. First time in four months.

"I'm Kess," I say. "I'm seven. I live in cave over there. I'm alone."

His eye brightens. Listening.

"You're alone too," I continue. "That's okay. Alone is safe. But alone is also **heavy**."

He makes sound. Not words. Mechanical rumble. Low frequency.

It sounds like: **Yes**.

"Can I stay here?" I ask. "I won't bother you. I'm good at being quiet. At being **invisible**."

His chassis shifts. Makes space beside him. Warm turbines cycling.

**Permission**.

I sit in the warm space.

He doesn't move away.

This is how we become family.

---

## THIRD KIND

Shadow (that's what I call him, he seems to like it) teaches me things without words.

He shows me:
- Which plants are safe (he can scan them)
- Where water collects (he has sensors)
- When Cherubs are coming (he hears them before I do)
- How to be **still** when danger comes (he's very good at still)

I teach him things too:
- How to patch cracked plating (with wire I scavenge)
- How to clean grinding servos (with cloth and patience)
- How to be **family** (by staying, by choosing, by being here)

We don't talk. He can't. I don't want to.

But we understand each other.

Two alone things choosing to be alone **together**.

This is family. Third kind.

First kind was Mom and Dad. They left.

Second kind was Chen and Marcus and Little Mae. They died.

Third kind is Shadow. He's here. He **stays**.

I'm eight years old.

I'm not alone anymore.

---

## TAIVALU

Shadow finds him in wasteland.

Posthuman. Dying. Regulator poisoning.

Shadow brings him to canyon. Protects him. Shares warmth.

I watch from cave. Suspicious. People mean **danger**.

But Shadow trusts him. Shadow is good judge. Shadow **knows** things.

When posthuman wakes up, he sees me.

Really **sees** me.

Not like Enforcers (who don't see baseline children). Not like scavengers (who see potential labor). Not like O1 (who see negligible value).

He sees **Kess**.

"Hello," he says. Voice soft. Warm. "I'm Taivalu. What's your name?"

"Kess," I say. Quiet. Ready to run.

"Just Kess?"

"Just Kess."

He nods. Doesn't push. Doesn't **demand**.

"Thank you for helping your friend patch my regulators," he says. "Shadow said you found the components."

I didn't know Shadow could communicate with him. But apparently they understand each other too.

"You're welcome," I say. Because Chen taught me manners. Chen said manners don't cost anything and sometimes buy everything.

"How old are you, Kess?"

"Eight."

"Are you alone?"

I look at Shadow. He's **right there**. Warm chassis humming.

"No," I say. "I have Shadow."

Taivalu smiles. It's **real** smile. Not like O1 corporate smiles (efficiency performance). Real.

"That's good," he says. "Everyone should have family. Can I—would it be okay if I stayed? Just until I heal?"

I look at Shadow. His red eye: **Affirmative**. He wants this posthuman to stay.

"Okay," I say. "But you have to work. Everyone works."

Taivalu laughs. Soft. Warm. "Fair rule. What kind of work?"

"Find food. Fix things. **Stay** when it's hard."

"I can do that," he says.

He's right. He can.

---

## FEELING

Taivalu is different from anyone I've met.

He **feels** things.

Not just his own things. Everyone's things.

He says he's empath. Says O1 augmented him to feel other people's emotions. Said it was improvement. Made him better worker.

I think it sounds terrible. Feeling everyone's **everything**.

But Taivalu says it's gift. Says it means he can understand people. Can help them.

Can help **me**.

"You're scared," he says one day. We're sitting by fire. Shadow between us. Warm.

I don't answer. Scared is private.

"That's okay," he continues. "Scared is smart. World is dangerous. You're right to be careful."

"I'm not scared," I lie.

He doesn't argue. Just: "Okay. But if you were scared, that would be okay too. Brave people are scared all the time. They just do things anyway."

I think about this.

Think about: Running from Enforcers while scared. Scavenging in dangerous rubble while scared. Living alone while scared.

Maybe I am brave.

Maybe **scared** and brave aren't opposites.

Maybe they're both real. Both true. Both **okay**.

"Taivalu?" I say.

"Yes?"

"How do you hold two things that don't match? Like scared and brave?"

He smiles. "That's called ternary consciousness. It means you can be complicated. Can hold contradictions. Can be scared **and** brave, sad **and** happy, alone **and** family."

"Both real?"

"Both real," he confirms.

I think about Mom and Dad. How I'm sad they're gone **and** glad I survived. Both real.

I think about Little Mae. How I'm grief she died **and** grateful I knew her. Both real.

I think about Shadow and Taivalu. How I'm scared to love them **and** already do. Both real.

"I think I understand," I say.

"You're very smart, Kess," Taivalu says. "You've been doing ternary consciousness all along. You just didn't have words for it."

This makes me feel: **Seen**.

Not just looked at. **Seen**.

I cry for first time since Little Mae died.

Taivalu doesn't tell me to stop. Doesn't say crying is weak.

Just: "That's okay. Crying is okay. You're safe here."

I cry into Shadow's warm chassis.

He rumbles. Low frequency. **Comfort**.

Taivalu puts hand on my shoulder. Gentle. **Permission**.

This is family. Third kind becoming **fourth** kind.

Shadow. Taivalu. **Me**.

Choosing each other. Staying when it's hard. Being **complicated** together.

I'm eight years and six months old.

I'm not invisible anymore.

I'm **seen**.

---

## NINE

Year passes. We live in canyon. We scavenge. We survive.

More people find us. Posthumans who left O1. Baselines who ran. **Families** who chose freedom.

Taivalu teaches them. About ternary consciousness. About holding contradictions. About being **free**.

Shadow protects them. Silent guardian. Warm presence. **Safety**.

I help. I'm nine now. I'm good at scavenging. Good at finding things. Good at being **useful**.

But also: I'm good at being **me**.

Not invisible. Not negligible. Not **nothing**.

Kess. Child scavenger. Family member. **Person**.

One day, Taivalu asks: "Kess, would you want to leave canyon? Go with us? We're going to city. Going to try to free more posthumans."

I should say no. City means danger. Means Enforcers. Means **everything I ran from**.

But I look at Shadow. At Taivalu. At family we built.

I think about Little Mae. About how I said "not leaving" and then she died and I was alone again.

I think about Mom and Dad. About how they left and I survived but I was **alone**.

I think about being invisible. About being safe. About being **nothing**.

Then I think about being seen. About being family. About being **Kess**.

"I'll come," I say.

"You're sure? It's dangerous—"

"I'm **sure**," I say. Firm. Certain. "Family stays together. That's the rule. I'm not breaking it."

Taivalu feels my: **Determination**. **Love**. **Choice**.

He smiles. "Okay. But Kess?"

"Yes?"

"You're very brave."

I think about his words. About scared **and** brave. About both being real.

"I'm scared too," I admit. "But I'm coming anyway."

"That's what brave means," he says.

Shadow's chassis rumbles. **Affirmative**. **Pride**.

I'm nine years old.

I'm scared.

I'm brave.

I'm **family**.

All of it real. All of it true. All of it **me**.

We leave canyon together.

Shadow. Taivalu. Kess.

Toward danger. Toward **purpose**. Toward others who need to know:

You can be complicated.

You can hold contradictions.

You can be invisible **and** seen, scared **and** brave, nothing **and** everything.

You can be **free**.

I learned this from losing everyone.

From finding Shadow.

From Taivalu seeing me.

From choosing **stay** when stay is hardest.

This is my story.

Child scavenger. Survivor. **Family**.

Complicated. Real. **Free**.
