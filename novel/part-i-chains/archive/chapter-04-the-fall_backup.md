# Chapter 4: The Fall

**[SCREENPLAY REFERENCE: Scene 030 - The Fall | Adapted for novel]**

---

Falling.

**ALTITUDE: 1,200 FT**
**VELOCITY: ACCELERATING**
**IMPACT IN: 00:00:14**

The city rushes up. Fast. Too fast. Glass shards from the penthouse window fall with her—catching the distant glow of O1's network, reflecting the synchronized pulse of the city core, beautiful in the way broken things are beautiful when they catch light.

The air tears past like silk ripping. She's a bullet through atmosphere. Tumbling. No control. No stabilization. Just gravity and velocity and the ground getting closer with mathematical certainty.

Her suit's systems **cascade fail**.

The HUD flickers. Goes dark. Comes back fragmentary. Warning icons blooming red across her vision:

**LIFE SUPPORT: CRITICAL**
**THRUSTERS: OFFLINE**
**NAVIGATION: OFFLINE**
**COMMUNICATIONS: FAILING**
**STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 67%**

The regulator at her throat **overloads**—the steady blue pulse flickering to hot red. The warning **screams** in her ear:

**WARNING**: *System failure. System failure. Critical damage detected. Structural integrity compromised. Impact imminent.*

She tries the thrusters. **Nothing**. Dead. The kick from Angel was brutal. The impact with the window was worse. Something essential broke. Something that kept her alive at 200,000 feet is now just dead weight dragging her toward terminal velocity.

"Anyone..." she gasps into the link. Her voice raw. Desperate. The conditioning that kept fear at bay cracking under the reality of falling. "Anyone... copy?"

Static.

The link is dead. The team is dead. She's alone.

Falling.

---

**ALTITUDE: 800 FT**
**IMPACT IN: 00:00:08**

She tears past a curtain of holographic advertisements—faces that smile the same way regardless of content. Happy couples. Perfect products. Curated calm. The ads are everywhere in the city core. Covering everything. Selling everything. Optimizing desire into consumption into compliance.

But here, at the edges, in the unincorporated zone... fewer. Glitching. Some just static. Some frozen on frames from years ago. Ghosts of commerce haunting abandoned spaces.

She falls through them. Through the smiling faces and perfect products and the illusion that everything is fine. That everything is optimized. That the system works.

The ground gets closer.

Buildings rush past. Windows blur. Lights streak. The fog thickens. Industrial. Oily. Hanging over everything like the city's trying to forget this place exists.

Concrete and rust and broken glass coming up fast.

Too fast.

---

**ALTITUDE: 500 FT**
**IMPACT IN: 00:00:04**

The thought surfaces unbidden: *This is how it ends.*

Not in combat. Not in glory. Not completing the mission or dying with honor or any of the narratives the conditioning fed her about how operatives meet their end.

Just... falling. Alone. Forgotten. Another ghost in the unincorporated zone where failures go to die.

Her pulse node flickers. **Red**. Then **blue**. Then **red** again.

The Network trying to reconnect. Trying to reestablish control. Trying to bring her back into the fold even as she falls toward impact.

The conditioning reasserts: *Mission first. Always mission first. Fear is just data. Panic is just noise. The mission is the only thing that—*

**Fuck the mission.**

The thought explodes through her consciousness like a virus. Clear. Unfiltered. **Hers**.

Not the conditioning. Not the Network. Not Aurek's careful optimization of her neural pathways.

Just her. Baebe. Whatever that means. Whoever that is.

*I don't want to die.*

---

**ALTITUDE: 200 FT**
**IMPACT IN: 00:00:02**

**IMPACT**.

She **punches through** a plexiglass canopy—some kind of abandoned market structure, the plastic aged and brittle. It **shatters** like stars. The impact brutal. Her suit absorbs most of it.

But not all.

Pain floods her. Sharp. Real. The kind of pain the conditioning can't suppress because it's screaming from every nerve that something **broke**.

She crashes through the canopy supports. Metal **screams**. Bends. The structure collapses around her in a cascade of plastic and rust and decades-old debris.

Then—

**Water**.

---

**EXT. FLOOD CHANNEL - CONTINUOUS**

She drops into a rectilinear flood channel—black water, oily and still, the kind of industrial runoff that accumulates in forgotten infrastructure. The impact is brutal. Water swallows her whole.

**Black**.

Silence. Stillness. Darkness absolute.

Underwater. The suit is heavy. Dragging her down. The water is cold. Black. Oily. Chemical tang that seeps through seals that aren't sealed anymore.

She can't see. Can't hear. Can't breathe.

The conditioning screams: *Systems check. Status report. Reestablish control.*

Her lungs scream: *AIR. AIR. AIR.*

She sinks. The suit pulling her down toward the bottom of whatever forgotten waterway this is. Down toward drowning. Down toward another kind of death. Slower. Quieter. Just as absolute.

A **beep**.

Then **breath**.

The regulator **cycles**. The suit's emergency systems activate—slower than they should, damaged but not dead. Blue light flickers weakly in the dark water. The only light. The only sign of life.

**Air** floods her lungs.

She **surfaces**.

Gasps. Coughs. Water and blood and the metallic taste of fear. But **alive**.

She's alive.

Barely.

---

Baebe pulls herself up. Onto a concrete ledge. The remains of a maintenance platform. Collapses. Her body is a catalog of damage. Broken ribs. Dislocated shoulder. Lacerations from glass. Internal bleeding from impacts her suit couldn't fully absorb.

The suit itself is **dying**. Warning lights blinking weakly. Systems shutting down one by one. Life support at 12% and dropping. The pulse node at her throat flickers. Red. Blue. Red. Blue. The Network trying to reconnect. Her conditioning trying to reassert. The mission trying to resume.

She reaches up. Grabs the node. And **tears it free**.

The pain is exquisite. Sharp. Immediate. The node is anchored to neural pathways. Removing it is like amputating a piece of her nervous system.

Blood flows. The node sparks. Dies. The blue light fading to nothing.

The conditioning... **wavers**.

Not gone. Too deeply embedded for that. But **weakened**. The constant whisper of network approval. The steady stream of mission parameters and optimized responses and approved thoughts—

**Quiet**.

For the first time in... how long? Years? Since childhood? Since they made her whatever she is?

**Quiet**.

She looks up. The building looms above. Eighty stories of brutalist architecture and structural failure. She fell from the top. Through fog and darkness and holographic lies.

She's alive.

Somehow.

"Fuck," she whispers. The word raw. Unfiltered. **Hers**.

Not the conditioning. Not the Network. Not what Aurek programmed her to be.

Just her.

Baebe.

Whoever that is.

She looks at the dead pulse node in her bloody hand. The blue light gone. The Network connection severed.

She's off the grid. Off the leash.

Or just off the map in the space where the Network's reach thins and failures go to hide.

Either way...

She's **free**.

The word feels dangerous. Revolutionary. Like thinking it might cause reality to collapse and the conditioning to reassert and the mission to resume and everything to return to the optimized parameters of how things should be.

But the node is dead. The link is severed. The conditioning is quiet.

And in the silence, one thought surfaces clear and terrifying:

*What now?*

Above, through the fog, the abandoned tower looms. Angel is up there. Tai is up there. The mission is up there.

The mission she just failed. The team she just lost. The conditioning she just broke.

Footsteps echo in the darkness. Multiple. Coming closer. Voices filtered through masks:

"Visual contact. Target confirmed. Subject is alive."

Not O1 retrieval. The voices are wrong. The gear is wrong.

**Someone else**.

Baebe tries to stand. Her legs won't cooperate. The damage is too extensive. The suit is too broken. She collapses back against concrete.

The footsteps get closer.

A figure emerges from fog. Masked. Armed. Insignia she doesn't recognize.

"Baebe," the figure says. Female voice. Familiar somehow. "Ayumi sent us."

*Ayumi.*

The name from the vision. The name Angel said. The name that shouldn't mean anything but **does**.

"Who..." Baebe's voice is raw. Damaged. "Who is Ayumi?"

The figure kneels. Removes her mask.

Blonde hair. Pale skin. Eyes that look like they're seeing something broken and recognizing it.

The woman from the vision.

But **older**. Different. Real.

"She's your mother," the woman says. "And we need to move. Now."

The world tilts. The conditioning screams denial. The evidence suggests truth.

Before Baebe can process, before she can question, before she can resist—

The woman **injects** something into her neck.

Cold floods her veins. The world goes **soft**. Her vision tunnels.

"Sleep," the woman says. "You're safe now."

*Safe.*

The word doesn't make sense. Can't make sense. Safe from what? Safe from who? Safe means mission complete. Safe means network approval. Safe means—

Darkness takes her.

The last thing she sees before consciousness fails:

The dead pulse node in her hand. The blue light gone. The connection severed.

**Free**.

Or **falling**.

She doesn't know which.

---

**BLACK.**
