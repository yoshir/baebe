# Chapter 1: The Drop

**[SCREENPLAY REFERENCE: Scene 010 - The Drop]**

---

Black.

The soft mechanical inhale of a pressurized bay. Not mechanical like grinding gears or whirring servos—mechanical like breathing machinery. Organic. Alive. The sound fills the darkness with presence, with weight, with the suggestion that something vast is drawing breath around her.

Baebe stands in the launch tube, sealed in composite alloy and waiting. The darkness is absolute. Total. The kind of darkness that exists in space, in the void between stars, where light goes to die. She's been standing here for six minutes. Waiting. Preparing. Letting her body sync with the mission parameters that scroll across her visual cortex in phosphorescent green:

**TARGET: T-ALPHA**
**LOCATION: i0 TOWER - SECTOR PRIME**
**ALTITUDE: 87,000 FT**
**MISSION: RETRIEVE ASSET ALIVE**

The numbers mean nothing yet. They're abstractions. Data. It's only when the tube opens and she falls that they'll become real—become velocity and distance and the brutal physics of a body plummeting through atmosphere.

The hum of life support surrounds her. The whisper of recycled air through graphene filters that scrub carbon dioxide and pump oxygen back into her lungs. The thrum of engines running silent—aneutronic fusion reactors dampened to near-invisibility, their heat signatures masked by active metamaterial cooling systems that radiate waste heat into carefully tuned non-detection bands. The stealth carrier doesn't exist. Not officially. Not on any grid or sensor array. Not on radar, not on thermal, not on quantum detection nets. It's a ghost ship carrying ghost soldiers toward a target that will pretend they were never there.

The darkness begins to resolve.

Not suddenly. Not with the dramatic flip of switches or the harsh flood of emergency lighting. Slowly. In increments so subtle she doesn't notice the transition until the stars are visible through the narrow viewport—pinpricks of light against infinite black. And below them, hanging in the void like something impossible: Earth.

Blue. White swirls of cloud. The curve of horizon cutting across her field of vision. It's beautiful in a way that makes her chest tighten, makes something behind her sternum ache with a feeling she has no name for. She's seen Earth from orbit forty-seven times before. This is deployment forty-eight. The beauty should have worn off by now. Become routine. Become data.

It hasn't.

The blue pulse at her throat flickers once. Quantum-entangled neural node checking her emotional response in real-time, flagging the unnamed feeling, comparing it against baseline parameters stored in distributed ledgers across the Network. The pulse finds nothing outside acceptable ranges. Relief +0.23. Anticipation +0.41. Mission focus +0.87. All within tolerances. Behavioral conditioning holds. The feeling passes, gets filtered through limbic dampeners, becomes irrelevant.

She turns her head—slow, controlled, the suit resisting the motion—and looks to her left. Through the narrow viewport of her launch tube, she can see the others.

Six more tubes. Six more soldiers. Six more weapons sealed in composite alloy and waiting.

**Agent 1** stands in his tube like a monument to survival. Mid-thirties. Scarred. The veteran. Plasma burns trace his left cheek—legacy of a drop that went wrong three years ago. Mission 34. Half the squad died. He didn't. He never does. Through his viewport, he catches her eye and nods once. Respect. Recognition. The silent language of people who've fallen together and lived.

**Agent 2** adjusts her harness for the third time. Mid-twenties. Nervous energy bleeding through perfect stillness. Her fingers tremble against the restraints—micro-movements her conditioning can't quite suppress. This is her first drop. First time falling from space. First time trusting cold-burning discs and stealth tech to keep her invisible. The fear is there—sharp and immediate—but she's here. Sealed in the tube. Ready. That counts for something.

**Agent 3** runs systems check in methodical silence. Late twenties. Calm. The kind of calm that comes from treating every mission like a physics problem with known variables and acceptable risk tolerances. His HUD flickers green. All nominal. Heart rate: 62 BPM. Oxygen saturation: 98%. Suit integrity: 100%. He's ready. They're all ready. The numbers say so.

**Agent 4**—silent, lethal, thirty-two years of compressed violence waiting to be released—stands perfectly still in his tube. Doesn't fidget. Doesn't check systems. Doesn't look at the others. He's already gone. Already dropped. Already at the target. His body just hasn't caught up yet.

Agents 5 and 6 are shadows in the peripheral tubes. Present. Accounted for. Weapons with heartbeats.

Seven agents. Seven tubes. Seven ghosts about to fall.

Baebe looks at each of them. Takes them in. Records their faces in the part of her memory that isn't monitored, isn't filtered, isn't reported back to the network. This might be the last time they're all together. Alive. Whole.

The thought passes. Gets filed. Becomes irrelevant.

**FLIGHT COMMS** crackles through her helmet—hushed, filtered through encryption layers thick enough to make the voice sound like something underwater:

"Ghost mode engaged. All systems nominal. We're dark. They don't see us. They won't see you."

Silence settles over the bay. The agents don't move. They're already gone. Already ghosts. Already not-there in the way that matters—erased from sensor arrays, scrubbed from thermal imaging, invisible to the grid that watches everything.

Baebe speaks. Her voice cuts through the silence—not loud, not harsh, just clear. Present. Real.

"I know you know this, but I'm gonna say it again."

She pauses. Lets the words land. They've heard it. They've practiced it during the seventy-two-hour mission prep cycle that stripped them down and rebuilt them into weapons. But repetition keeps them alive. Ritual matters. The reminder matters. The truth matters.

"Once we hit atmosphere, we're on our own. No backup. No extraction. No rescue. If you fall behind, you're gone. If you hesitate, you're dead. The tower sees everything. We're ghosts. We don't exist. Make it count."

The agents nod. Small movements. Helmet micro-adjustments. They know. They always know. But hearing it matters.

**HACKER**—voice filtered through quantum-locked encryption, calm and tech-focused, somewhere far away and impossibly close—speaks next:

"Stealth carrier's signature is clean. Your suits are running cold—no thermal bleed, active heat sinking to 4 Kelvin. Comms are quantum-entangled, impossible to intercept. Metamaterial cloaking engaged. Atmospheric entry in three. Two. One. You're ghosts. Nobody sees you. Nobody tracks you. You don't exist on any spectrum they're watching..."

Baebe whispers to herself—so quiet the comms don't pick it up, so quiet it's just breath and intention:

"You fall faster than the feed can follow. That's how you stay clean."

The words are ritual. Mantra. She's said them before every drop. Every mission. Every time she steps into the void and trusts physics to be kinder than gravity wants it to be. The Network monitors her heart rate—notes the micro-spike of anticipation (+0.31)—compares it against baseline parameters and finds nothing outside acceptable ranges.

Good girl. Perfect soldier. Weapon in human form.

**COMMAND**—dry, metallic, stripped of everything human—begins the countdown:

"Execute in T-minus ten."

The number echoes through the bay. Each syllable a weight. Each syllable a choice made and unmade and made again.

"Nine."

The tubes hum. Low frequency vibration that travels through her boots and up her spine. Activation sequence initiated.

"Eight."

Agent 2's breathing accelerates. Not panic. Not yet. Just the body recognizing what's about to happen and refusing to believe it.

"Seven."

Baebe counts with them. Synchronizes. Becomes part of the machine. "Six."

The blue pulse at her throat flickers faster. Network connection intensifying. Ready to monitor. Ready to record. Ready to judge whether she's still useful or broken beyond acceptable parameters.

"Five."

The hatch beneath her is still closed. Still solid. Still the only thing between her and 87,000 feet of empty air.

"Four."

Agent 1 checks his weapon—muscle memory, ritual, the thing you do when there's nothing left to do.

"Three."

The tubes activate fully. The hum becomes a roar becomes a feeling that lives in the bones.

"Two."

This is the moment. The edge. The place where you either trust the tech or you don't and either way you're falling because the choice was made long before you stepped into the tube.

"One."

The hatch beneath her—solid composite alloy designed to hold vacuum—begins to IRIS OPEN. Mechanical petals peeling back to reveal the void. The Earth. The fall.

"Execute."

---

## THE DROP

The tubes activate.

The sound is visceral—not loud but *felt*, a low-frequency thrum that bypasses the ears and goes straight to the gut. The hatches beneath each tube iris open in perfect synchronization. Mechanical flowers blooming toward death.

The void appears.

Cold. Absolute. The Earth below. Waiting.

**Agent 4** drops first. Straight out the bottom of his tube. No hesitation. No sound. No explosion. Just gravity doing what gravity does—pulling mass toward mass with patient, inevitable force.

A thin disc—no bigger than a dinner plate—activates beneath his boots the moment he clears the ship. VAPR-derived transient composite. Metamaterial coating engineered to redirect thermal radiation into non-visible bands—heat dissipated as infrared in the 5-8 micron range, invisible to both sensors and naked eye. No glow. No signature. No trace.

The disc doesn't burn. It just exists—a ghost platform providing just enough drag to prevent immolation while staying too small for radar, too cold for thermal imaging, too fast to intercept.

He drops. Gone. A weapon released. In six hours, stress-release triggers will shatter the disc into sub-micron fragments that scatter on the wind. No evidence. No forensic signature. The mission was never here.

**Agent 1** drops. Straight down. The disc activates. Silent. Invisible. Undetectable. Another ghost.

**Agent 3** drops. Calm. Methodical. Falling like it's a systems check he's run a thousand times before.

**Agent 2** drops—her fear invisible now, compressed into the space between heartbeats. The disc activates beneath her boots—metamaterial already redirecting reentry heat into invisible wavelengths. She's falling. Alive. The fear follows.

Agents 5 and 6 drop. Shadows falling through shadows.

Six agents. Dropped straight out the bottom of the ship. Six transient platforms. Six ghosts. Too small to track. Too silent to hear. Too invisible to stop.

Baebe's tube activates.

The hatch beneath her irises open.

She drops.

---

## FALLING

She drops straight out the bottom of the ship.

The transition is immediate—solid deck to infinite void, support to freefall, contained to uncontained. No gentle push. No dramatic leap. Just the sudden absence of ground and gravity pulling her down at 9.8 meters per second squared.

The disc activates beneath her boots the moment she clears the hull. Metamaterial surface flickering through thermal management protocols—redirecting kilojoules of reentry heat into invisible infrared bands. Silent. Invisible. Complete stealth.

And she falls.

Sudden. Immediate. Inevitable.

Inside her helmet, the sound is DEAFENING.

Her heartbeat THUMPS—loud, too loud, a drum solo played on her ribs. Her breathing RASPS—harsh pulls of recycled air through filters that scrub carbon dioxide and pump oxygen back into her lungs. The suit's life support HUMS—constant, insistent, alive. The comms CRACKLE—static and encrypted voices bleeding through quantum-locked channels.

All of it amplified. Isolated. Trapped inside the suit with nowhere to go. The only sounds in a silent void.

Outside: nothing. The void doesn't carry sound. The thin air at 87,000 feet is too sparse to vibrate with anything resembling noise. The fall is silent. Stealth.

Inside: everything. Her body. Her fear. Her mission. All of it loud and inescapable.

The G-forces hit.

Not gradually. Not with warning. Just sudden crushing weight that drives her vision to tunnel, forces blood toward her feet, makes her organs feel like they're trying to exit through her spine. The suit compensates—electroactive polymer muscles contracting around her legs and abdomen, pressure bladders inflating with magneto-rheological fluid, forcing blood back toward her brain against 7 gees of deceleration. Barely. Just barely enough.

She's falling. Fast. Too fast.

The transient disc beneath her feet remains utterly dark. No visible emission. Stealth metamaterial doing its job—redirecting reentry heat into the 5-8 micron band where neither eye nor sensor can detect it. Creating just enough atmospheric drag to prevent immolation while staying small enough, cold enough, invisible enough to evade all detection.

The other agents fall around her. Six invisible platforms. Six ghosts. Six human meteors cutting through the stratosphere in perfect silence and darkness.

No fire. No heat signature. No visible trace. Absolute stealth.

Ghosts.

Her heads-up display snaps to life on her visor—phosphorescent green text overlaying the impossible view of Earth rushing up to meet her:

**TARGET: T-ALPHA**
**ALTITUDE: 87,000 FT**
**DESCENDING**
**VELOCITY: 1,247 M/S**
**ETA: 00:03:47**

The numbers tick down. Altitude dropping. Velocity increasing. The city grows. Closer. Closer. Real.

Her pulse node flickers once—blue, steady, monitored—then something else. A glitch. Micro-stutter in the quantum entanglement. For a fraction of a second, the edges of her vision warp as if reality itself lags a frame. A Resonant Fade—those impossible moments when the world splits into overlapping eigenstates and you exist in superposition. Quantum decoherence failing. The wave function refusing to collapse.

For a moment, she's between. Neither here nor there. Neither falling nor caught. Neither particle nor wave. Neither real nor unreal.

She blinks.

The glitch resolves. Decoherence completes. Reality snaps back to a single eigenstate. The fall continues.

The atmosphere catches.

Reentry begins.

Externally: nothing. No sound. The thin air at this altitude doesn't carry it. The drop remains silent. Stealth.

Inside her suit: CHAOS.

The suit SCREAMS. Warning alarms BLARE—high-pitched urgent sounds that drill into her skull. The heat builds. Fast. The temperature gauge spikes:

**REENTRY HEAT SHIELD: 87%**
**TEMPERATURE: 2,847°F**
**WARNING: CRITICAL**
**STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 94%**

The heat is IMMENSE. Plasma-level temperatures battering the suit's outer layer—ablative coating burning away in controlled strips, sacrificing itself molecule by molecule to keep her from becoming a meteor streak. The suit holds. Barely. The transient disc beneath her remains dark—metamaterial surface temperature spiking to 700°C but radiating everything into non-visible bands. Invisible. Stealth. Undetectable even at peak thermal load.

The reentry is VIOLENT. Turbulent. The air screams past—but she can't hear it. Only the suit. Only the alarms. Only her heartbeat hammering against her ribs like something trying to break free.

Micro-thrusters fire. Stabilizing. Tiny bursts of compressed gas adjusting her trajectory in real-time—compensating for wind shear, atmospheric turbulence, the chaotic mathematics of a human body falling through air. The sound of them is LOUD. Inside her helmet. Inside her head.

**AGENT 2**—voice filtered through comms, panicked, LOUD—breaks protocol:

"I'm off trajectory! I'm—"

**AGENT 1**—urgent, commanding:

"Recalibrate! Now!"

Baebe watches through her HUD as Agent 2's trajectory corkscrews—spiraling off course, micro-thrusters failing to compensate, the fall becoming uncontrolled. Then the suit's AI kicks in. Emergency protocols. The trajectory straightens. She's back on target. Alive. Barely.

Baebe speaks. Calm. Present. The voice that cuts through panic:

"We're good. We're all good. Stay focused."

The heat builds. The suit screams. The fall continues. Silent outside. Loud inside. Absolute stealth.

The other agents fall around her. Six invisible platforms converging. Six ghosts. Six weapons. Too small to track on radar. Too cold to register on thermal. Too dark to see. Too fast to stop.

The tower.

The i0 Tower.

An 800-story glass monolith that sings with low-frequency light—a beacon in the darkness, a god's monument to control, the heart of the distributed quantum network that keeps eight billion people connected and compliant. Thirty-two petabytes of human experience processed every second. Every pulse node. Every neural contact. Every emotion logged, filtered, optimized. The tower is the synapse. The city is the brain.

Their target.

Their mission.

Their death sentence.

The city rushes up. Streets become visible. Buildings resolve from abstraction into concrete reality. The grid becomes real. The city is a circuit board—lights pulsing in synchronized patterns that suggest distributed consciousness, suggest emergent intelligence, suggest something vast watching through a million eyes at once.

They're the disruption. The glitch. The virus introduced into a closed system.

"All agents. Status check."

**AGENT 1**—filtered, calm:

"Green. On target."

**AGENT 3**—steady, mechanical:

"Green. Systems nominal."

**AGENT 2**—breathless but alive:

"Green. I'm— I'm okay."

The others check in. All green. All alive. For now.

"Remember the protocol. Stealth. Speed. Precision. We're ghosts. We don't exist."

**AGENT 1**:

"Copy that, Ghost One."

The city fills her vision. The tower dominates. 800 stories of glass and light. A vertical city within a city. The seat of power. The architecture of control made physical.

**ALTITUDE: 12,000 FT**
**ETA: 00:00:45**

The numbers tick down. The fall accelerates. The mission begins.

The squad angles toward the structure. Six cold coronas converging on a single point. Six ghosts. One target. One mission.

The tower looms. Massive. Impossibly tall. The kind of structure that shouldn't exist but does anyway—proof that when you have infinite resources and perfect control, physics becomes negotiable.

Baebe watches it grow. Watches the landing zone resolve from concept to coordinates to the actual place where she'll touch down or die trying.

The city rushes up to meet them.

And somewhere in the tower—somewhere in those 800 stories of glass and light—Tai is waiting.

T-Alpha.

The asset.

The target.

The reason seven people just fell from space.
