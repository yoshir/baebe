## AWAKENING

A FACE.

Extreme close-up. Eyes SNAP OPEN.

Blonde hair. Pale skin. Wide with panic. Shock.

She GASPS.

An impossible gasp. Like there isn't enough air in the world to fill her lungs. Like she's drowning on dry land. Like she's been holding her breath for a lifetime and the first breath back is agony.

Her mouth opens. Wide. Her chest heaves. She gasps again. Harder. Deeper. Desperate.

There isn't enough air. There will never be enough air.

---

The vision slams into Baebe's consciousness like a spike through her visual cortex. Not memory. Not hallucination. Something else. Something breaking through.

A face.

Blonde hair catching light. But wrong. The features won't resolve. Pixelated. Fragmented. Like her conditioning is fighting to suppress recognition—surveillance systems freezing memory before it can surface into understanding. The woman's face flickers—there, not there, almost familiar, almost—

Mother?

The thought surfaces—unauthorized—and drowns immediately. The conditioning crushes it. Absolute. Not mother. Can't be mother. Mothers don't exist in the Network's taxonomy of Baebe's life. She was made. Not born. Made.

But the face persists. Blonde hair. Eyes that should mean something. A name trying to surface from neural substrate that's been rewritten too many times, recognition trying to break through suppression.

The woman gasps. Her mouth opens. Eyes wide with something that looks like terror or breakthrough or both.

---

EYEBALL - MACRO VIEW

Inside her eye. The pulse of light. The iris contracts. Expands. Neural firing patterns shifting—something trying to surface, something else pushing it down. The pulse reveals. Hides. Reveals again.

The gasp becomes a pulse. A rhythm. A war inside her skull.

---

THE WOMAN'S BODY

Close on her side. Sweat traces geometric patterns down pale skin. Her body breathes—not just lungs but skin, every pore opening to exchange heat and moisture with air that feels too thick, too present, too real. The pulse of light reveals curves. Shadows. Heat radiating. Sweat glistening like oil on water. Her blonde hair catches the light and transforms it.

The gasp becomes a breath. A pulse. A light.

---

HER FACE

Eyes closed now. Lips parted. Sweat on her forehead forming rivulets that follow the contours of bone beneath skin. Her breathing slows. The pulse of light reveals it with clinical precision—chest rising, falling. Slower. Slower. The return to baseline after sympathetic nervous system overload.

The gasp becomes silence. A breath. A pulse.

---

BASS POUNDS. SYNTHESIZERS SCREAM.

The vision slams into focus mid-sequence—Baebe catching only the end. The blonde woman's body arching. Every muscle rigid. Frequencies colliding at critical mass. Nanobots swarming frantically through blood and tissue. Chemicals exploding in cascading light.

Her face contorted. In pain. In ecstasy. In something beyond both.

Everything approaching singularity—

THE WOMAN'S EYES SNAP OPEN.

SHE SCREAMS.

A raw, primal sound. Pure terror. Pure release. Pure power. The scream of someone surfacing from drowning or waking from death or remembering something that should have stayed forgotten.

The scream cuts through everything.

## THE LANDING

Baebe's eyes snap open.

The vision fragments. Dissolves. Becomes irrelevant data that her conditioning files away for later analysis that will never come.

Wide. Alert. Present. Here. Now.

ALTITUDE: 2,000 FT
ETA: 00:00:12

The building rushes up through the fog. Not abstract. Not vision. Real. Solid. Dangerous.

"Deploy wings." Her voice steady. Present. The vision—whoever that woman was, whatever she was experiencing—is gone. Only the mission remains. "Prepare for insertion."

---

The suit responds.

Fractal structures DEPLOY from the armor's back plate—not rigid mechanical wings but something organic, something that looks grown rather than built. Adaptive material that shifts and responds to airflow in real-time. Graphene-carbon nanotube composites woven into fractal patterns that maximize surface area while minimizing mass. Smart materials encoded with shape-memory properties that read air pressure and adjust geometry on microsecond timescales.

They're not rigid. They're alive. Almost.

The wings EXTEND—silent, graceful, the metamaterial surface shimmering as it adjusts to atmospheric conditions. They catch the air. The sensation is immediate—sudden drag that shifts her trajectory from falling to gliding. From ballistic to aerodynamic. From victim of gravity to something that flies.

Agent 1 glides beside her—his wings fully deployed, fractal surfaces adjusting to turbulence with fluid grace.

"Beautiful," Agent 1 says, voice filtered through quantum encryption. "Just... beautiful."

"Focus," Baebe responds. "We're not here to enjoy the view."

But she does. For a moment. The view is breathtaking.

---

The wings respond to micro-movements. She banks left—barely shifts her weight—and the fractal surfaces reconfigure, creating differential drag that turns her glide path with precision that feels telepathic. The wings read the air. Respond to pressure gradients too subtle for human perception. Shift and adapt in patterns that suggest artificial intelligence running locally in the material itself.

They're not just wings. They're extensions of her nervous system. Almost sentient. Almost alive.

The sensation is extraordinary. Not falling. Flying. The city sprawls below—circuit board patterns stretching to the horizon, lights pulsing in synchronized rhythms that suggest distributed consciousness at urban scale. But at the edges... darkness. Gaps in the grid. Zones where the lights flicker or don't light at all. The unincorporated zone. Their target. The sky above is darkening toward dawn, stars fading as Earth's rotation brings the sun toward the horizon.

She's flying. Really flying. Human body + metamaterial wings + physics = temporary transcendence of terrestrial limitation.

The other agents glide around her. Three more. Four total. Silent. Deft. Perfect. They move in formation—a quartet in the sky, coordinated through the link and years of training that makes individual thought unnecessary. The squad is an organism. The wings are organs. The mission is metabolism.

ALTITUDE: 500 FT
APPROACHING TARGET

The building resolves through fog. Not O1's gleaming spires. Something older—a brutalist skyscraper from the 2020s, eighty stories of steel and concrete and broken glass. A monument to pre-superintelligence ambition, now abandoned. And at the top... wrong. The crown tilted, structural failure caught mid-collapse, the upper fifteen floors slumped at an impossible angle.

They're close. Too close. The building fills her vision—rust-stained facades, shattered windows, the tilted section cantilevered over nothing. A relic that should have been demolished but wasn't, because in the unincorporated zones, nothing gets demolished. Things just stop.

"Adjust approach," Baebe commands. "Slanted surface. Watch your footing."

They angle in. Fast. No choice. Four agents shifting trajectory—wings reconfiguring for treacherous approach, bodies aligning for impact on angled glass and rust, momentum that won't bleed off cleanly.

They hit the slanted crown.

Feet first on fifteen-degree slope.

Wrong angle. Wrong surface. Gravity pulling sideways.

Agent 2 slips—boots scrambling on moss-covered façade. Agent 1 grabs her harness. Arrests the slide.

"Steady," Baebe says. Her own boots mag-locked to corroded steel. Barely holding. "Wings dissolve in thirty. Move now."

The winged suits DISSOLVE.

The fractal material breaks down at the molecular level—transient polymers receiving chemical signal that initiates programmed decomposition. The wings fade. Dissipate. Sublime into vapor that disperses in fog. Gone in seconds. No evidence. No forensic signature.

But now they're exposed. Four bodies clinging to fifteen-degree slope, eighty stories above ground, fog swirling. The building groans under their weight like it's considering whether to finish its decades-long collapse right now.

"Down," Baebe orders. "Penthouse level. Fifty feet."

They move. Rappelling down slanted glass and steel. Mag-boots finding purchase on window frames, structural beams, anything solid. The city's distant glow creates halos in mist but reveals nothing close.

Just concrete. Rust. The groan of stressed metal. The precarious angle that makes every movement feel like falling.

Agent 3 reaches it first: a ledge. Horizontal. The penthouse roof terrace—one of the last levels before structural failure. Solid footing.

They drop onto it one by one. Feet on level ground. Finally stable.

"We're down," Baebe says. Her pulse node flickers—steady, monitored, approval maintaining tactical lock. "Begin insertion."

The agents move. Weapons ready. Systems engaged. Neural contacts active. Conditioning solid. They're here. They're ghosts. They're ready.

Fog swirling. The skeletal frames of penthouse gardens that stopped growing before Baebe was born.

And from inside the building—

Sound.

Not mechanical. Not human either. Something between. A frequency that shouldn't exist but does—alive. It resonates in the chest and makes the air feel thick, makes thoughts scatter like the sound is reaching into her skull and rearranging neurons. Something spreading through tactical certainty, dissolving it. If she unfocuses her overlay, she can almost see it: frequencies painting the fog in living colors.

Tai's sound.

The thing he does. The ability that made him valuable and then made him dangerous and then made him something O1 couldn't control.

Frequency manipulation. Emotional resonance. The capacity to make people feel things the network says they shouldn't feel. Human things that melt conditioning.

Empathy virus, the briefing called it.

The thing that could break conditioning if you listened too long.

Agent 3, voice tight: "Is that—?"

Baebe: "Target confirmed. Weapons ready. ROE Condition Three—capture preferred, elimination authorized."

She draws her weapon. A plasma rifle. Old tech. Reliable. The weight of it familiar, comforting, the thing that makes her something other than a conditioned victim falling toward her own execution.

Makes her the executioner instead. Precise.

The sound continues. Rises. Pulls. Something wrapping through the fog.

Something in her chest responds—unauthorized. Something the conditioning can't quite suppress.

She forces it down.

Mission first.

Always mission first.

"We're going in."
