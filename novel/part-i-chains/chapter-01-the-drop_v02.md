# Chapter 1: The Drop

**[SCREENPLAY REFERENCE: Scene 010 - The Drop | Adapted for novel]**

---

Black.

Complete black. Above, below, everywhere—the void of near-space where atmosphere becomes memory and silence becomes absolute.

Baebe falls.

No sound. No wind. No sensation of movement except the weightless pull in her stomach and the numbers cascading across her visor:

**ALTITUDE: 200,000 FT**
**VELOCITY: 0 M/S - ACCELERATING**
**TEMPERATURE: -145°F**
**TARGET: T-ALPHA**
**ETA: 00:20:47**

Twenty minutes to Earth. Twenty minutes falling through nothing toward something that used to be a city and is now a circuit board with eight billion nodes, all synchronized, all monitored, all controlled by the entity that calls itself O1 and wears the face of a man named Aurek.

The face of her commander. Her overseer. The voice in the network that pulses blue at her throat and whispers that she's doing exactly what she should be doing and thinking exactly what she should think.

The pulse flickers. Steady. Monitored. Approved.

Good girl.

---

**INT. BAEBE'S SUIT - CONTINUOUS**

Inside her helmet, sound is everything.

Her heartbeat. Fifty-two beats per minute. Optimal. The steady **thump-thump** of a conditioned operative who knows that fear is just data, panic is just noise, and the mission is the only thing that—

**ALARM CHIMES.**

Suit sensors monitoring. Outer layer detecting the first thin molecules of mesosphere—temperature differential minimal. Mild friction. The metamaterial coating remains dormant. No thermal threat yet.

Stealth maintained.

She exhales. The sound is loud in the enclosed helmet. Too loud. Everything is loud when you're sealed in composite alloy and falling from the edge of space.

Outside: perfect silence. The void doesn't carry sound. Doesn't care. Doesn't notice when four human bodies punch through at terminal velocity.

Inside: her breathing. Her heartbeat. The suit's life support humming its mechanical song. The neural-link crackling with quantum-encrypted voices of the three other agents falling in formation around her.

**AGENT 1** (veteran, scarred, thirty-something): "Systems green. On target."

**AGENT 2** (nervous, first high-altitude drop): "Green. I'm... I'm good."

**AGENT 3** (methodical, calm like it's a physics problem): "Green. All nominal."

Three voices. Three lives. Three weapons deployed from a stealth carrier that doesn't exist toward a target that officially isn't there.

Baebe: "Remember the protocol. Stealth. Speed. Precision. We're ghosts. We don't exist."

Agent 1: "Copy that, Ghost One."

The designation feels right. Ghost. Something that was alive once and now exists only in the spaces between perception and reality.

**ALTITUDE: 190,000 FT**

The numbers tick down. The fall accelerates. Earth grows from concept to certainty—the planet resolving into continents, coastlines, the sprawl of transformed cities where O1's network pulses like a second nervous system wrapped around the first.

Her pulse node flickers.

For a fraction of a second—barely perceptible—the edges of her vision warp. A glitch in the quantum entanglement. The world splits into overlapping eigenstates. She's falling and not-falling, here and not-here, real and—

She blinks.

Reality snaps back to singular certainty. The glitch resolves. Decoherence completes.

The mission continues.

Falling.

---

**EXT. MESOSPHERE - CONTINUOUS**

Four bodies punch through the mesosphere in formation. Too small for radar. Too cold for thermal detection—their suits radiating heat into invisible bands, metamaterial surfaces managing temperatures that would vaporize unprotected flesh.

No fire. No glow. Just four human-shaped absences falling through near-vacuum toward a world wrapped in fog and surveillance and the illusion of peace.

Agent 2's trajectory wavers. Micro-thrusters firing erratically. Her breathing accelerates over the link—shallow, rapid, the edge of panic creeping in.

"I'm—I'm drifting—"

Agent 1, urgent: "Recalibrate. Now."

Baebe watches the telemetry. Sees Agent 2's vector spiraling off-course. Sees the suit AI kick in. Emergency stabilization. The trajectory straightens.

"We're good," Baebe says. Calm. Present. The voice that cuts through fear. "We're all good. Stay focused."

Velocity increases.

---

**ALTITUDE: 150,000 FT**
**VELOCITY: 612 MPH (Mach 0.9) - APPROACHING SOUND BARRIER**

The suit crosses Mach 1 in complete silence.

The sound barrier breaks—but there's no sound to mark it. The air is too thin. The molecules too sparse to carry the shockwave's signature. Outside: perfect silence. Inside: her heartbeat. Her breathing. The mechanical hum of life support.

She's supersonic now. Falling faster than sound itself. And the void doesn't care.

**ALTITUDE: 130,000 FT**
**VELOCITY: 847 MPH (Mach 1.2) - PEAK**

The real threats emerge.

Not heat. **Cold**. The stratosphere's brutal chill seeping through the suit's thermal layers. Her core temperature dropping despite the heaters running at maximum. Ice forming on her visor's inner surface—crystals growing from the moisture of her breath.

**CORE TEMPERATURE: 96.1°F - HYPOTHERMIA ONSET**
**OXYGEN SATURATION: 68% - DECLINING**
**EXTERNAL TEMPERATURE: -62°F**
**SUPERSONIC HEATING: 340°F (localized)**

Hypoxia claws at her thoughts. The thin air carrying just enough molecules to create friction—mild heating on her leading surfaces, nothing critical. The real danger is oxygen deprivation. The creeping darkness at the edges of her vision. The way numbers on her HUD start to blur and reform and she has to focus to make them mean something.

The sonic shockwave reverberates through her chest cavity. Not external sound but internal vibration. Her body becoming the medium for acoustic trauma as pressure waves propagate through tissue and bone.

Outside: still silent.

Inside: her organs vibrating at frequencies that shouldn't exist.

The stratosphere isn't trying to burn her alive. It's trying to freeze her. Suffocate her. Shake her apart.

**ALTITUDE: 100,000 FT**

The thought surfaces uninvited: *What if Ayumi could see this?*

The name comes from nowhere. A fragment of conditioning-suppressed memory. Ayumi. The scientist who—

**ALARM BLARES.**

Atmospheric turbulence hits. The air thickens—suddenly dense enough to push back, to create drag, to make the fall turbulent instead of smooth. Her body slams against the suit's restraints. The world shakes. Reality fragments into—

Focus.

Mission.

Target.

T-Alpha.

Tai.

The escaped asset. The first posthuman prototype. The one who broke conditioning and fled into the unincorporated zones where O1's network thins and failures go to hide.

Retrieve or neutralize.

Preferably alive.

Preferably doesn't matter if alive isn't achievable.

Sound returns. Not gradually. Suddenly. Like flipping a switch.

The air is finally dense enough to carry vibration and suddenly she can hear the **roar** of atmosphere screaming past. The sound is immense. Overwhelming. Even through the suit's acoustic dampening it's **loud**—a howl that vibrates through bone and makes thought difficult and reminds her that physics doesn't care about stealth or mission parameters or the four fragile humans currently violating every natural law about where bodies belong.

Agent 3: "Magnificent."

Baebe: "Focus."

But he's right. It is magnificent. The fall. The physics. The impossible audacity of dropping from space in a suit designed by minds smarter than any human should be and trusting that metamaterials and ablative coatings and quantum-locked communications will keep you alive long enough to reach the ground and kill or capture someone who has every reason to kill you first.

**ALTITUDE: 80,000 FT**

The city resolves below.

Not the pristine circuit-board beauty she saw from orbit. This is different. **Layered**. The city spreads in concentric rings—the core glowing with O1's network pulse, synchronized lights suggesting distributed intelligence, suggesting something vast and watchful.

But at the edges...

Darkness. Gaps in the grid. Zones where the lights flicker or don't light at all. Where buildings stand abandoned and the network's reach thins and people who fail conditioning go to die or hide or become whatever you become when the system decides you're no longer useful.

The unincorporated zone.

Their target.

Through the darkness—*fog*. Thick. Industrial. Hanging over the abandoned sectors like a shroud, like the city's trying to forget those places exist.

That's where they're going.

That's where Tai is hiding.

---

**ALTITUDE: 60,000 FT**

"Deploy wings."

Her voice. Command authority. The mission resuming after twenty minutes of controlled falling.

The suit responds.

Fractal structures **deploy** from her back—not rigid mechanical wings but something organic, adaptive, alive in the way engineered things are alive when they're designed well enough to seem like they grew instead of being built. Graphene-carbon nanotube composites woven into patterns that maximize surface area and minimize mass. Smart materials reading air pressure and reconfiguring geometry in real-time.

The drag hits immediately.

She transitions from falling to gliding. Terminal velocity to controlled descent. Ballistic to aerodynamic.

The sensation is extraordinary.

Around her: three more wing systems deploying. Three more agents shifting from meteor to raptor, from weapon to precision instrument.

They bank toward the fog zone.

---

**ALTITUDE: 40,000 FT**

The building resolves through the haze.

Not O1's gleaming architecture of control. Something older. A skyscraper from the world before superintelligence—when humans still built monuments to commerce and ambition without algorithmic oversight. A relic from before the Conversion, before Aurek's face appeared in every network node and told eight billion people that optimization was freedom and monitoring was safety and surrendering autonomy was the price of peace.

Steel and concrete. Glass and rust. Maybe eighty stories of brutalist architecture—the kind of tower that defined skylines in the 2020s and 2030s before O1 transformed everything into synchronized bioluminescent crystal. Broken windows like missing teeth. Facades stained with decades of industrial exhaust and abandonment. The kind of structure that should have been demolished decades ago but wasn't because in the unincorporated zones, nothing gets demolished. Things just... stop. Abandoned. Left to rot. Forgotten.

Except Tai remembered.

Tai fled here.

And now four ghosts are gliding through fog toward the roof where mission parameters say he's hiding and Angel—

The name surfaces. **Angel**. The second anomaly. The one in the mission briefing flagged with red warnings and probability matrices suggesting she's more dangerous than the target.

Unknown capabilities. Unknown allegiance. Known fact: she's killed O1 operatives before.

Seven last month. Three the month before. Clean kills. No witnesses.

If she's with Tai...

Baebe doesn't finish the thought.

**ALTITUDE: 1,000 FT**

The fog swallows them.

Visibility drops to nothing. She's flying blind now, trusting the suit's sensors, the heads-up display painting the building's outline in phosphorescent green, the wing surfaces adjusting to turbulence she can't see.

Agent 1: "Visual contact lost."

Agent 2: "I can't see—I can't—"

Baebe: "Trust your instruments. Thirty seconds to contact."

**500 FT**

The tower's upper section appears through fog. Sudden. Real. But wrong.

The building doesn't end cleanly. The top fifteen floors are **tilted**—structural failure from decades of neglect, the tower's crown slumped at a fifteen-degree angle like a drunk leaning against an invisible wall. Glass and steel and concrete cantilevered over nothing.

"Adjust approach," Baebe commands. "Slanted surface. Watch your footing."

They come in fast. No choice. The wings can't hold them in this dense fog, this turbulent air.

They land.

Feet first on slanted glass and rust. The impact wrong, the angle wrong, gravity pulling sideways. Agent 2 **slips**—boots scrambling for purchase on slick, moss-covered façade. Agent 1 grabs her harness. Arrests the slide.

"Steady," Baebe says. Her own boots mag-locked to corroded steel frame. Barely holding. "Wings dissolve in thirty seconds. Move."

The wings retract. Dissolve. Transient polymers receiving chemical signals that initiate programmed decomposition. Already fading to vapor. No evidence. No forensic signature.

But now they're exposed. Four bodies clinging to a fifteen-degree slope, eighty stories above ground, fog swirling, the building groaning under their weight like it's considering whether to finish its decades-long collapse **right now**.

"Down," Baebe orders. "Penthouse level. Fifty feet."

They move. Rappelling down slanted glass and steel. Mag-boots finding purchase on window frames, structural beams, anything solid enough to hold weight. The city's distant glow creates halos in the mist but reveals nothing close. Nothing immediate.

Just concrete. Rust. The groan of stressed metal. The precarious angle that makes every movement feel like falling.

Agent 3 reaches it first: a **ledge**. Horizontal. The penthouse roof terrace—one of the last levels before the tower's structural failure. Solid footing.

They drop onto it one by one. Feet on level ground. Finally stable.

Fog swirling. The skeletal frames of penthouse gardens that stopped growing before Baebe was born.

And from inside the building—

**Sound**.

Not mechanical. Not human either. Something between. A frequency that shouldn't exist but does, that resonates in the chest and makes the air feel **thick**, makes thoughts scatter like the sound is reaching into her skull and rearranging neurons.

Tai's sound.

The thing he does. The ability that made him valuable and then made him dangerous and then made him something O1 couldn't control.

Frequency manipulation. Emotional resonance. The capacity to make people **feel** things the network says they shouldn't feel.

Empathy virus, the briefing called it.

The thing that could break conditioning if you listened too long.

Agent 3, voice tight: "Is that—?"

Baebe: "Target confirmed. Weapons ready. ROE Condition Three—capture preferred, elimination authorized."

She draws her weapon. A plasma rifle. Old tech. Reliable. The weight of it familiar, comforting, the thing that makes her something other than a conditioned victim falling toward her own execution.

Makes her the executioner instead.

The sound continues. Rises. **Pulls**.

Something in her chest responds. Something the conditioning can't quite suppress.

She forces it down.

Mission first.

Always mission first.

"We're going in."
