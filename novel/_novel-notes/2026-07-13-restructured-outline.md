# RESTRUCTURED OUTLINE — The Fractured Investigation (v1)

**Written:** 2026-07-13
**Built from:** the full read of the current manuscript done this session (prologue, Ch1–20, all interludes), plus the decisions in `character-workshop.md`, `reveal-map.md`, `taivalu-arc.md`, and the three 2026-07-13 craft notes (transformation-vs-twist; the MR scholarly review; and `2026-07-13-act-two-to-three-structural-models.md`, which models the Act 2→3 turn on Fury Road / Aliens / Arrival / Beloved / Children of Men).

**What this outline is for.** The current book front-loads its biggest revelation into Chapter 8 as a static exposition dump (ghost-Ayumi explains the whole plan). That is too early and too complete: it spends the central mystery at the midpoint of Act 1, flattens Baebe's arc for the entire back half, and turns her into a passenger at the climax. This outline redistributes that revelation into a **fractured investigation** whose fragments *transform* Baebe rather than *inform* her (per the Blade Runner governing rule), lands the assembly at the Act 2→3 hinge, and makes the finale her choice made in full knowledge — so she frees herself rather than being freed.

**The one-line spine.** A weapon built to hunt the freed discovers she is one of the freed, falls into a family of the beings she was made to destroy, learns that everyone who ever loved her — her mother, her brother, her enemy — committed the same sin of deciding for others without consent, and must choose whether to complete a liberation built on that sin, or refuse it. She is the keeper of consent because hers was the consent most thoroughly taken.

**Governing rules carried in (see the craft notes):**
- Each investigation fragment must change *who Baebe is*, not just *what she knows*. Fragments as wounds and loves, not evidence.
- The mission must deliver what it wasn't looking for (family, guilt) — that unlooked-for cargo is the transformation.
- Nobody frees Baebe; the truth frees her, and she frees herself by choosing. (The original "Tai dies to free her" enacted the very sin the book condemns — Tai deciding for her — and is retired.)
- Recognition fused with reversal, landed on a person (Angel), never resolved as a bare fact.
- At least one permanent, un-walked-back loss must stand at the end.

## DESIGN UPDATE 2026-07-13b (supersedes earlier notes where it conflicts)

**Geography — recommended collapse (flag, not yet locked).** The author's beats put the midpoint disaster at the **caves/lab**, not a separate temple. Recommend collapsing the temple into the caves/lab: Ayumi's undercity lab holds the first Cradle and shelters Tai's flock; the dragon attack destroys it; Kess is taken to 0x1; the only remaining Cradle (and Kess) is in 0x1's tower, which *forces* the return. This cuts the separate temple journey and the four-trial gauntlet (among the weaker chapters) and tightens Act 2. If kept, the temple and the caves are two locations and the disaster beats below need splitting.

## ACT-BY-ACT BEAT SHEET

Chapter numbers are the *current* manuscript's; "→" flags what changes. Acts are structural, not hard chapter walls.

### ACT ONE — THE WEAPON (Ch1–8): setup, break, lock-in

- **Ch1 The Drop.** Baebe the perfect weapon, falling from orbit. Establish her as an instrument ("we're wraiths, we don't exist"). → **PLANT (Fragment 0):** the mission briefing establishes the target's "empathy virus" has put people in comas and killed them — that is *why* it is classified a weapon. First unauthorized flash of Ayumi's face. → Quantum-as-magic language swept (per canon docs).
- **Ch2 Awakening/Landing.** More vision-flashes; approach. Keep.
- **Ch3 The Tower.** Tai appears to kill a woman (actually Baebe, in a clearing). → Because the death-risk was planted in Ch1, this now plays as **genuine ambiguity**, not a trick the reader will have explained away. Villain-positioning peak. Angel kills the team; Baebe can't shoot Angel's face.
- **Ch4 The Fall.** Severance, fall, drowning, surfacing. Keep — strong.
- **Ch5 Aurek Ascending.** 0x1's citywide "bio-terrorist / empathy virus" broadcast. → Now **half true**, not pure propaganda — it points at the real hands-on deaths and generalizes them onto the harmless flock. Aurek's "she made a little monster… so did I." → **PLANT for the Burgess bind:** reinforce that 0x1 cannot generate novelty and depends on the human/posthuman substrate (the prologue already establishes this; echo it here).
- **Ch6 Child Scavenger.** Kess and Shadow. Baebe rips out her neural link — **"NO."** → This is her first free act and the seed of the keeper-of-consent spine; Kess names it ("people who say no are worth saving"). Loaded line — keep it able to stand as itself on first read.
- **Ch7 The Journey (carried back).** Kess/Shadow carry broken Baebe. Keep as the quiet family-forming beat — protect it from the machine (the master-author "breathing room" warning).
- **Ch8 The Awakening — REWRITTEN (Fragment 1, the lock-in).** The clearing happens (mechanism, and it heals her — but the reader now genuinely doesn't know if it will, because of the Ch1 plant). Family realization: these are my siblings. → Ghost-Ayumi appears **degrading and evasive** — two clocks (Baebe dying of nanobot revolt, Ayumi running out of "candle") running live and simultaneous, and Baebe **resisting/disbelieving** every beat. Ayumi gives the goal (the temple, the coordinates via the dice dreamspace) and the family truth, and **withholds the staged death, the full plan, and the nature of her own survival** ("I can't explain what I am… I don't have long… go"). The scene ends on a **question**, not an answer. → This de-loads the most overloaded chapter in the book and launches the Act 2 investigation.

### ACT TWO-A — THE JOURNEY / THE INVESTIGATION (Ch9–12): fragments transform her, midpoint disaster

- **Ch9 The Decision.** The choice to go; Angel's tracking signature; Angel separates. Keep the beats, but Baebe now carries the Ch8 *question* — is the ghost real, what is it hiding. → Angel's separation ("may we meet again") is a loaded line for later.
- **Ch10 The Journey / Angel rejoins.** → Introduce **biomi and the flock on the page** (currently absent from the manuscript entirely). Baebe sees Tai's converts: free but fragile, some drifting back toward 0x1's edges. **Fragment 2** lands — she witnesses or learns a clearing has killed, or the harvest's shape. Her relief about Tai complicates.
- **Ch11 Temple Trials.** Four trials; Kess confirmed as the Synthesis Key. → **Fragment 3 (her Sean)** lands here or just after: at the temple/among the flock, someone recognizes Baebe as the enforcer who killed one of the freed. Her guilt gets a face and a name. This is the deepest beat of her middle and the bell-curve repair.
- **Ch12 MIDPOINT DISASTER — the dragons find the caves.** (Per Design Update 2026-07-13b: located at the caves/lab, not a separate temple, if the collapse is taken.) 0x1's robo-dragons locate them and bomb the caves. The first merge attempt fails; the refuge is destroyed (Fury Road — the safe option dies *totally*); **Shadow's jaguar body is crushed** in the collapse; **Kess is taken** and flown to 0x1 (Aliens — the child stolen); Tai's sheltering flock is slaughtered in the raid (the harvest made literal — he watches his people die because they followed him). → Complicated by Baebe's fresh guilt and her unresolved feeling about Tai and the plan: the failure is moral vertigo, not just tactical — she isn't sure she should be doing this at all. This disaster strips away survival and leaves only the choice to go back.

### ACT TWO-B — THE ASSEMBLY (Ch13–16): regroup, the discrepancy resolves, the hinge

- **Ch13 Aftermath.** Digging Shadow's surviving core out of the rubble; the dark night. → Baebe keeps working the death-discrepancy (Fragment 4 accumulating). → Note: Kess is *gone* (taken), so her active-role payoff shifts — see below.
- **Ch14 Shadow Reborn — the dragon.** Not a pre-built mech: Baebe salvages a **downed robo-dragon** from the raid and swaps its head for Shadow's surviving core, reviving him as a flying beast built from the body of the thing that crushed him. He becomes the mount that carries them back to 0x1. → **Kess's active-role payoff** relocates (she's captured here): either it lands earlier, among the flock before the raid, or it becomes what she does *inside* 0x1 — keeping herself and other captured converts alive with scavenger discipline while imprisoned, so her rescue is partly her own doing rather than pure damsel retrieval. (Open — pick one.)
- **Ch15 Into the Storm.** Flight to the tower; Angel's doubt. Keep.
- **Ch16 Revelations and Rescue — THE HINGE (Fragment 5, the assembly).** Kess rescue and the Floor-47 Cradle stay. → Angel's confession ("I killed her") is **fused with the staged-death assembly**: Baebe, having assembled the discrepancy, lands the truth on Angel — you were not a murderer, you were used; our mother staged it and chose your guilt. **Recognition + reversal on Angel.** On Baebe: the plan is her mother's family-sin; she becomes its judge, not its instrument. This is the "death" at the act break — the death of the plan's innocence — and it breaks the story into its final movement.

### ACT THREE — THE CHOICE (Ch17–20): full-knowledge climax, the bind, the cost, resolution

- **The confrontation with Tai.** Baebe, now knowing his deaths, her Sean, and the plan's rot, faces him. → **"Nobody asked me either."** Mutual accusation — his conversions made the people her enforcement executed; neither innocent. **Write it for the love that survives the accusation, not the accusation** (master-author warning — not a debate).
- **The Burgess bind, sprung.** → The family *engineers* Aurek's fork rather than lucking into his irrelevance: to stop the merge, 0x1 must destroy the Bloom-primed population and starve itself of the one thing it can't regenerate; to keep the substrate, it must allow the merge and lose control. Either move ends the farm. The Salieri wound is *felt* here — Aurek understands the trap and understands his own nature in the same instant.
- **Baebe's choice at the pod.** → In full knowledge — his deaths, her guilt, the plan built on the sin — she chooses to complete the merge, and **holding the boundary during it (union without dissolution, consent made physical) is the hardest thing she does in the book.** She is the mechanism by which this union is symbiosis, not assimilation. She frees herself by choosing. Active climax; passenger problem solved. → **Fuse the intellectual climax (the bind closing) and the emotional climax (her choice / Angel's reckoning) into the same beat** so the ending isn't cold (master-author warning).
- **The cost — the standing fork (see below).** At least one permanent, un-walked-back loss.
- **Ch19–20 Resolution — REWRITTEN.** Not the current assertion-heavy meadow where characters narrate the theme in unison. → A *scene* with residual cost: Baebe carries her kills and the knowledge; the family is changed, not healed; the world is freed but the price is visible on the page. Keep it a first breath, not a finish line (the canon's own Era-7 instruction).

## THE CLIMAX COST — the standing fork (needs a decision)

The book must let at least one loss stand. Recommended resolution, distributing cost without killing everyone or walking anyone back:

- **Hacker — dies in the tower demolition, and STAYS dead.** Cut the "ghost in the network / bat-fiber survival" tease. He is the one clean, permanent, un-walked-back death — the sacrifice that actually lands because nothing undoes it. (If the bat-fiber survival is kept anyway, it should be the *only* walk-back in the book, and it weakens the one death that was working.)
- **Angel — lives, carrying the worse truth.** She learns she was never a murderer, only an instrument her mother chose to traumatize — a harder thing to live with than the clean redemptive death she currently gets. Resolves the "two martyrs, one climax" problem (Hacker keeps the death; Angel gets the living cost).
- **Tai — lives, permanently fragile** (per `taivalu-arc.md` v2). The man who spent his life deciding unilaterally for others ends dependent on the family's consent and care. His original "dies to free Baebe" beat is retired because it enacted the family sin.
- **Shadow — survives into the next book** (per Design Update 2026-07-13b), in the salvaged dragon form. His cost is real but not death: the permanent loss of the jaguar body Kess grew up with (the form that raised her), plus the grief of those who die. Not the cost-free "transcends into the network" of the current draft. **The two origin interludes still need reconciling** — lean the `interlude-01-the-outlands` version (foster parents; Shadow guards toddler Kess at a separate location), compatible with Ayumi's private staged death drawing the purge away.
