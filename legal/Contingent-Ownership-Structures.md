# Contingent Ownership Structures - Alternative IP Protection Models

**Version:** v.20251114-2021  
**Last Updated:** 2025-11-14  
**Status:** Planning Document (Requires Attorney Review)

---

## Overview

Contingent ownership structures allow IP to remain with you until certain conditions are met, at which point ownership transfers to the company. This provides protection during early stages while allowing eventual transfer if the company succeeds.

---

## How Contingent Ownership Works

### Basic Concept

**IP stays with you until trigger condition → Then transfers to company**

```
You (IP Owner) → Owns BAEBE IP
    ↓
    [Condition Not Met] → IP Stays With You
    ↓
    [Condition Met] → IP Transfers to Company
    ↓
Optimal Anarchy → Owns IP (After Transfer)
```

### Key Principle

**Ownership transfers only if/when conditions are met. If conditions aren't met, IP stays with you.**

---

## Common Trigger Conditions

### 1. Funding Milestones

**Example:** "IP transfers to company only after $1M in funding raised"

**Structure:**
- You own IP initially
- Company gets license until funding milestone
- Once $1M raised, IP transfers to company
- If milestone never reached, IP stays with you

**Benefits:**
- ✅ Protects IP until company proves it can raise capital
- ✅ Clear trigger (dollar amount)
- ✅ Investors understand structure

**Considerations:**
- What if company raises $500K? (IP stays with you)
- What if company raises $2M? (IP transfers)
- Need clear definition of "funding"

---

### 2. Revenue Milestones

**Example:** "IP transfers only after first $100K in revenue"

**Structure:**
- You own IP initially
- Company gets license until revenue milestone
- Once $100K revenue achieved, IP transfers
- If milestone never reached, IP stays with you

**Benefits:**
- ✅ Protects IP until company proves viability
- ✅ Based on actual business performance
- ✅ Aligns with company success

**Considerations:**
- What counts as "revenue"? (gross, net, specific sources?)
- What if company pivots? (milestone may never be reached)
- Need clear revenue definition

---

### 3. Time-Based Triggers

**Example:** "IP transfers only after 24 months of operations"

**Structure:**
- You own IP initially
- Company gets license for 24 months
- After 24 months, IP transfers to company
- If company fails before 24 months, IP stays with you

**Benefits:**
- ✅ Simple and clear
- ✅ Time-based (easy to track)
- ✅ Protects during early stage

**Considerations:**
- What if company needs more time?
- What if company succeeds early? (still transfers at 24 months)
- May not align with actual performance

---

### 4. Milestone Combinations

**Example:** "IP transfers when company raises $1M OR achieves $100K revenue OR operates for 24 months"

**Structure:**
- Multiple trigger conditions
- IP transfers when ANY condition is met
- Provides flexibility

**Benefits:**
- ✅ Multiple paths to transfer
- ✅ Flexible structure
- ✅ Protects until company proves viability

**Considerations:**
- More complex to track
- Need clear definitions for each condition

---

### 5. Performance-Based Triggers

**Example:** "IP transfers only if company achieves specific milestones (e.g., first product launch, first customer, etc.)"

**Structure:**
- You own IP initially
- Company gets license until milestones achieved
- Once milestones met, IP transfers
- If milestones never met, IP stays with you

**Benefits:**
- ✅ Based on actual performance
- ✅ Aligns with company goals
- ✅ Protects until company executes

**Considerations:**
- Need very clear milestone definitions
- What if milestones change? (company pivots)
- May be subjective

---

## Hybrid Models

### License + Contingent Assignment

**Structure:**
- You own IP initially
- Company gets exclusive license
- License includes automatic assignment if conditions met
- If conditions not met, license continues (or terminates)

**Benefits:**
- ✅ Best of both worlds
- ✅ License protects company operations
- ✅ Assignment protects you until conditions met
- ✅ Clear path forward

**Example:**
"Company receives exclusive license to use IP. License automatically converts to ownership assignment upon [condition]. If condition not met within [timeframe], license terminates and IP reverts to licensor."

## Implementation Considerations

### 1. Clear Definitions

**Critical:** Define trigger conditions precisely

**Funding Milestone:**
- What counts as "funding"? (equity, debt, grants?)
- Gross or net? (after fees?)
- From which sources? (all investors or specific?)
- When is it "raised"? (committed, received, closed?)

**Revenue Milestone:**
- What counts as "revenue"? (gross, net, specific products?)
- From which sources? (all revenue or specific streams?)
- When is it "achieved"? (earned, received, recognized?)

**Time Milestone:**
- When does "operations" start? (incorporation, first hire, first sale?)
- What counts as "operations"? (active business, minimum activity?)
- How to handle pauses? (suspended operations, pivots?)

### 2. Documentation Requirements

**What You Need:**
- Written agreement defining conditions
- Clear definitions of all terms
- Mechanism to verify conditions met
- Process for transfer when triggered
- Documentation of transfer (assignment agreement)

### 3. Transfer Process

**When Condition Met:**
1. Verify condition met (documentation, proof)
2. Execute IP assignment agreement
3. Record transfer (update copyright, file documents)
4. Update company records
5. Notify relevant parties

**Assignment Agreement Should Include:**
- Description of IP being transferred
- Consideration (if any)
- Representations and warranties
- Effective date
- Signatures

## Advantages

### For You
- ✅ IP protected until company proves viability
- ✅ Clear trigger for transfer
- ✅ Protects if company fails early
- ✅ Can negotiate terms

### For Company
- ✅ Can use IP for business (via license)
- ✅ Clear path to ownership
- ✅ Aligns with company success
- ✅ Investors understand structure

### For Investors
- ✅ IP will transfer if company succeeds
- ✅ Clear expectations
- ✅ Protects early stage
- ✅ Standard structure

---

## Disadvantages

### Complexity
- ⚠️ More complex than simple licensing
- ⚠️ Requires careful drafting
- ⚠️ Need to track conditions
- ⚠️ Potential disputes over conditions

### Uncertainty
- ⚠️ Unclear when transfer happens
- ⚠️ May complicate future deals
- ⚠️ Investors may prefer certainty

### Potential Issues
- ⚠️ What if condition partially met?
- ⚠️ What if company pivots?
- ⚠️ What if definitions unclear?
- ⚠️ Disputes over whether condition met

---

## When to Use Contingent Ownership

### Good For:
- Early stage companies (pre-revenue, pre-funding)
- When you want protection but eventual transfer
- When company needs to prove viability first
- When investors want clear path to ownership

### Not Good For:
- When you want to keep IP permanently
- When company is already established
- When you want maximum simplicity
- When investors demand immediate ownership

## Comparison to Licensing Model

| Aspect | Licensing Model | Contingent Ownership |
|--------|----------------|---------------------|
| **Initial Ownership** | You | You |
| **Company Rights** | License | License (until transfer) |
| **Final Ownership** | You (permanent) | Company (after trigger) |
| **Protection Level** | High | Medium-High |
| **Complexity** | Medium | High |
| **Investor Appeal** | Good | Good |
| **Flexibility** | High | Medium |

## Sample Agreement Structure

### Section 1: Initial Ownership
- IP owned by you
- Company receives license
- License terms defined

### Section 2: Trigger Conditions
- Clear definition of conditions
- Multiple conditions (if applicable)
- Verification requirements

### Section 3: Transfer Process
- When condition met
- How to verify
- Assignment process
- Effective date

### Section 4: Failure to Meet Conditions
- What happens if condition not met
- License termination (if applicable)
- IP reversion rights

### Section 5: Representations and Warranties
- You own IP
- Right to grant license/assignment
- No third-party claims

### Section 6: General Provisions
- Governing law
- Dispute resolution
- Entire agreement
- Modifications
